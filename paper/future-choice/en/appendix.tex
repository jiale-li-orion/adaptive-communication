\section{Proof Details}
\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Information structure and robust continuation}
Let $w\in\Worlds(h_t)$ fix a compatible latent initial state and subsequent exogenous/task-revision trajectory. The simulator transition $F$ and owner-scoped observation process $H$ then induce $\gamma_w=\operatorname{Run}(h_t,a,\pi,w)$; any randomness not explicitly represented in $w$ must be included in its exogenous continuation. At each decision epoch, $\pi$ sees its own actions, current time, and evidence that has actually arrived. Pending queries, deliveries, and acknowledgments may alter future transitions without supplying their outcomes in advance. A failed attempted transmission is allowed to yield a different observation in different worlds; it is not grounds for conditioning the decision \emph{before} that observation arrives.

For a finite scenario tree, the robust feasibility recursion is an OR over legal current actions and an AND over observations reachable after their transitions:
\begin{equation}
V(h)=\bigvee_{a\in\mathcal A(h)}
\bigwedge_{y\in\mathcal Y(h,a)}V(h^{a,y}),
\end{equation}
with terminal values determined by the operational completion/deadline predicate. Crucially, $h^{a,y}$ is an observation history with its remaining compatible world support, not a revealed world identity. Different worlds producing the same $y$ stay in one child node. An empty continuation caused by a task revision or deadline expiry cannot silently delete an obligation from the evaluator. The finite scenario tree is the paper's declared exact-reference support; the recursion does not assert field-scale universal robustness.

The quantifier distinction can be stated without introducing a new semantic utility. A robust causal policy satisfying every compatible world implies that each world admits a completion when future states are known in advance:
\begin{equation}
\bigl(\exists\pi^{\rm na}\,\forall w\;\operatorname{Done}(\pi,w)\bigr)
\Longrightarrow
\bigl(\forall w\,\exists\pi_w\;\operatorname{Done}(\pi_w,w)\bigr).
\end{equation}
The reverse implication fails when indistinguishable worlds require different actions before evidence arrives. Thus a perfect-information or per-world physical oracle may refute feasibility but cannot certify a common causal completion.

\subsection{Replayable lower certificates}
Consider a current legal history $h_t$ and action $a$. A lower certificate stores a continuation witness $\pi_c$ together with a declared validity domain $D(c)$. By construction, replay validation checks that (i) every action in $\pi_c$ is legal under the observation history on which it is taken, (ii) the policy branches only after an observation distinguishes histories, and (iii) every hard obligation induced on each covered compatible branch is completed before its operational deadline. The certificate may set $L_t(a)=1$ only if its declared world-support domain covers \emph{all} of $\Worlds(h_t)$ and its execution/resource/time assumptions still hold. Under those conditions $\pi_c\in\Choices(h_t,a)$, proving $L_t(a)=1\Rightarrow V^*(h_t,a)=1$.

The important restriction is non-anticipativity. A world-indexed open-loop trace is not a valid lower certificate unless the traces agree on every prefix with the same legal observations. This is why a perfect-information physical solution is an upper-bound aid, not a constructive lower certificate.

\subsection{Optimistic upper tests}
Let $P(h_t,a)$ denote the real continuation-feasibility problem and let $\widetilde P(h_t,a)$ be a relaxation obtained only by deleting constraints, shortening delays, increasing resources, or granting additional information. Every real feasible continuation is then feasible in $\widetilde P$. Hence
\begin{equation}
\widetilde P(h_t,a)\text{ infeasible}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
P(h_t,a)\text{ infeasible},
\end{equation}
which establishes the soundness of $U_t(a)=0$. The converse does not hold: an optimistic relaxation may be feasible even when no causal real policy exists.

\subsection{Hall deficits as necessary resource bounds}
In one compatible world, expand each remaining terrestrial window into unit-capacity opportunity slots. Let $\Ob_w$ be its residual obligations, $N_w(S)$ the slot neighborhood of subset $S\subseteq\Ob_w$, and $\nu_w$ the maximum bipartite matching cardinality. Standard matching--min-cut duality gives
\begin{equation}
\Delta_w=|\Ob_w|-\nu_w
=\max_{S\subseteq\Ob_w}\bigl(|S|-|N_w(S)|\bigr).
\end{equation}
Under the frozen adapter's unit-service contract, each terrestrial slot can complete at most one obligation. Therefore any physical completion requires at least $\Delta_w$ additional non-terrestrial deliveries in that world, even in the optimistic model that grants perfect world knowledge and ignores the cost of obtaining it. If the remaining backup budget $B$ permits at most $B$ such deliveries and $\Delta_w>B$ for some $w\in\Worlds(h_t)$, the robust continuation is impossible. A full matching or $\Delta_w\le B$ does not prove $L=1$: matching relaxes sensing/acknowledgment dynamics and may prescribe incompatible actions in worlds with the same observation history. If one future semantic packet could satisfy several obligations simultaneously, applying this unit-service Hall deficit would be unsound; that adapter would require a separate, proved optimistic coverage relaxation. The implemented flow/min-cut is used as a scoped structural certificate, not as a replacement for the exact AND/OR oracle.

\subsection{Dependency-local preservation}
Let $c$ be a component certificate with dependency set $\mathcal D_c$, compatible-world support $\Worlds_c$, resource interval, and validity boundary $t_c^{\rm valid}$. Suppose an event changes global legal history from $h$ to $h'$. Two distinct monotonicity rules apply. A positive replayable completion witness is preserved by support narrowing $\Worlds(h')\subseteq\Worlds(h)$ if the witness still covers the new support and the event changes neither action legality nor any task/resource/transition dependency it relies on. A negative impossibility conclusion certified by a specific counterexample world $w_c$ remains conclusive only if $w_c\in\Worlds(h')$ and its bound assumptions are intact. Removing $w_c$ may make the remaining support feasible: monotonicity of a positive witness cannot be applied to a negative verdict.

For either certificate polarity, reuse also requires that the current time stay inside the validity interval, that its resource budget remain in the certified range, and that no changed dependency invalidate the structural witness. A component-level matching/conflict cache may survive even when the global support-level feasibility verdict does not. These conditions delimit a sound reuse decision, rather than asserting that every observed event can be ignored.

The partition-safety boundary requires that no newly legal opportunity edge join a reused component to another component. The current transition model is monotone in future opportunity edges: time and resource use remove edges, while execution does not create an unseen terrestrial opportunity between previously unrelated components. The implementation nevertheless checks overlap between rebuilt and reused slot sets; otherwise it repartitions or rebuilds rather than claim a sound global certificate.

\subsection{Deadline-set MST upper bound}
For the external routing adapter, fix a deadline threshold $d$ and let $S_d$ contain all unserved customers whose deadlines are at most $d$. Any route that serves every node in $S_d$ before $d$ induces a connected walk spanning the current UAV position and all points in $S_d$. The length of any such connected walk is at least the Euclidean minimum spanning tree length over those points. Therefore
\begin{equation}
t + \frac{\operatorname{MST}(\{x_t\}\cup S_d)}{v} > d
\end{equation}
is sufficient to prove that no real route can meet all deadlines in $S_d$. The test ignores battery limits, charging detours, future return-to-depot requirements, and other constraints; all omissions make the relaxation more optimistic, so a negative result remains sound.

\section{External Cohort Construction and Statistics}
\label{app:external}

The external N=10 study separates correctness from statistical scale. We first scan seeds using only the four released ordinary heuristics. A seed enters the constructive pool if at least one heuristic already completes all customers, returns to the depot, incurs zero tardiness, and takes no infeasible action. FutureChoice and exact continuation are not used for selection. The first 21 constructive seeds form the exact-audited cohort; the first 100 form the scale-confirmatory cohort, so the correctness core is an exact prefix of the scale panel rather than a different sample.

For every reached action frontier in the 21-instance core, we compare the FutureChoice mask against an independent exact-continuation mask; the 924 reached frontiers have zero mismatch. The 100-instance panel is used for paired outcome statistics rather than repeating every exact audit. For binary outcomes we report Wilson intervals. Paired rate differences, tardiness changes, and energy changes use a fixed-seed paired bootstrap; zero-tardiness discordance additionally uses the exact two-sided McNemar test.

The external-paper-matched 50-episode panel serves a different purpose. Its layout seeds are generated by the released repository's own evaluation protocol, namely the sequence produced by \texttt{np.random.default\_rng(999)} for 50 episodes. Only six of those 50 layouts have an ordinary constructive zero-tardiness witness. We therefore run FutureChoice only on those six for hard-feasibility comparison and report the full 50-episode native/depth-$4$ results as distribution context. We do not interpret an unlabelled layout as hard-infeasible merely because an ordinary heuristic fails to produce a witness.

\section{Benchmark Validity and Model-Spectrum Boundary}
\label{app:benchmark}

The emergency benchmark is frozen independently of the method. Source/task/evaluator construction, the 200/200/150 coordinate split, deterministic baselines, execution-evaluator mutation tests, and the model-spectrum subset are all tracked as separate artifacts. Operational-Conformance and Interactive-Decision cases remain in the release even when ordinary rules solve them. Future-Choice-Stress is allowed to be empty; an empty stress stratum weakens a method-necessity claim inside the emergency benchmark but does not invalidate the benchmark itself.

The frozen LLM subset contains 30 test coordinates evaluated under two context modes. It is explicitly a model-spectrum diagnostic. Its role is to test whether the benchmark exposes model/runtime behavior that differs from the deterministic baseline landscape; it is not used as evidence that FutureChoice requires an LLM or that the proposed method improves language-model reasoning. Runtime failures, replay failures, and token/call/latency costs are preserved rather than silently dropped.
