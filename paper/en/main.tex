\begin{abstract}
When a mountain-site backhaul fails, the network keeps executing communication state that was
installed before the failure. Unacknowledged records keep filling the few recovery access
batches, and a dense sampling configuration that can no longer be countermanded keeps draining
a battery overnight. We study where, and on what evidence, these two forms of \emph{persistent
installed state} can be terminated, for a battery-plus-solar LoRaWAN Class~A deployment with an
autonomous gateway, a cellular primary and a rate-limited BeiDou short-message backup. One
decision problem---local termination under partial evidence---covers both: a node holding only
its clock, battery, cache, recent access receipts and install-time bounds decides to retain or
release a record and to hold or relax a configuration, under no-false-release, energy-survival,
locality and no-false-completion constraints. A record's termination time is its business
deadline, locally certain; the rule is bit-identical to standard Bundle-Protocol per-record
expiry, and its \emph{cross-segment placement} decides the gain: source-local expiry raises
outage on-time delivery $202\to434$ and 2.52-fold over ten seeds, whereas suppressing expired
sends at the gateway alone back-pressures access ($-8$). A configuration's termination time is
not locally observable, because it depends on mission revisions travelling the failed path and
on return-slot geometry held at the gateway. A clock night-guard suffices when the outage aligns
with sunset and energy is ample; under overcast skies it leaves 20--31 nodes to discharge
fatally. Preloaded local revert bounds remove these deaths without reducing aggregate yellow
obligation delivery; a fixed 8~h TTL also does so in both tested phases. A proposed
\emph{delivery-bounded lease} seeks to derive the bound from legally available mission and
return-slot information. Current experiments supply the future downgrade time offline and do not
validate that online derivation or command-carried installation. A formative LLM study locates an
interface fault: LoRa access receipts read as backhaul state. All claims come from deterministic, repository-reproducible
runs; end-to-end and cross-model agent validation is future work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Consider a field node running an authorised dense sampling profile. While the cellular backhaul
is up, the centre sends the configuration and the node applies it at its next LoRaWAN Class~A
receive window. The backhaul then fails for sixteen hours, and three things keep happening with
no control path to stop them. The node keeps sampling and transmitting at the dense rate,
drawing energy the battery may not have through the night, while a downgrade authored after the
failure cannot reach it. Records already cached at the node are, under the standard
hold-until-acknowledged edge discipline, retransmitted oldest-first on every narrow access
opportunity, including records whose business deadline has passed. The centre and gateway
cannot tell whether missing data were never sampled, sit at a node, reached the gateway, or
merely lack a return slot, nor whether a command is queued, in transit or applied. The network
is not stuck: \emph{installed state is still executing}, spending energy, access slots and
metered short-message bytes on work that can no longer meet any obligation.

This is a communication-systems failure with a specific shape: \textbf{the termination of a
persistent installed state depends on a control/acknowledgement path that has itself failed}.
SateRIoT observes that lossy satellite windows waste COTS-radio retransmissions and redesigns
link estimation and multi-hop sharing around that measured behaviour~\cite{sateriot}; semantic
HARQ reuses the mature HARQ primitive but changes its error criterion to match a new
communication object~\cite{razavivideo}. In the same spirit we ask which ordinary mechanism
already terminates each kind of installed state, and where a genuine gap remains once the
ordinary mechanisms are applied fairly.

\subsection{Two kinds of persistent installed state}
A \textbf{record state} is an unacknowledged sample in the node cache, occupying a slot in every
future access batch until acknowledged or discarded. A \textbf{configuration state} is the
node's current sampling/reporting profile, installed by an applied command, which drives energy
and airtime until a newer command or a local condition ends it. Both are created while connected
and both outlive the connection. They differ in what evidence terminates them: a record's useful
lifetime ends at an absolute business deadline the source computes from the public mission
cadence, whereas a configuration's useful lifetime ends when the data it produces can no longer
be delivered---a fact depending on mission \emph{revisions}, which reach the field over the
failed backhaul, and on the geometry of future return opportunities, which the gateway and not
the node holds. This asymmetry, made precise in \S\ref{sec:problem}, is the paper's technical
centre.

\subsection{What strong ordinary mechanisms already settle}
We first assemble deliberately strong baselines with the same legal information: energy-aware
feed-forward sampling, an omniscient offline packer, rolling-horizon MPC, max-coverage/recency
backup packing, earliest-deadline-first and obligation-greedy queues, piecewise non-prescient
mission views, and symmetric resource relaxations. Over the policy families tested,
re-optimising collection, scheduling or packing under fixed resources yields no robust
on-time-delivery gain; the large fixed-resource gains require added backhaul capacity or energy.
We report this as a scoped negative result. Two ordinary termination mechanisms also work well:
per-record expiry, where releasing a cached record at its business deadline is standard
Bundle-Protocol lifetime management~\cite{rfc9171}, to which a prior ``certificate-driven
purge'' proves bit-identical; and a local clock/energy guard, where a node-local rule relaxing
dense sampling at dusk removes overnight deaths whenever the outage aligns with sunset and the
battery is adequate. We credit both as mature primitives.

\subsection{The residual}
The experiments expose two placement effects. Suppressing expired records at the
\emph{gateway} while leaving the source cache untouched withholds the acknowledgement: the
record continues to fill the node's access batch and on-time delivery moves $202\to194$.
Source-local expiry removes this retained state. For configurations, the clock guard acts only
at dusk, leaving \cFiveNightfloorDeadA--\cFiveNightfloorDeadB deaths in two overcast scenarios.
Earlier preloaded revert times avoid these deaths. A short 4~h TTL gives up
\cFiveTtlFourLostB yellow deliveries in the longer dense phase, whereas a fixed 8~h TTL avoids
deaths and preserves the aggregate yellow delivery in both phases. A bound derived from energy
feasibility also avoids deaths but gives up \cFiveEnergyLostA and \cFiveEnergyLostB deliveries. These observations
motivate studying how to select and install a bound before disconnection; they do not establish
an increment over ordinary leases. In particular, a downgrade authored after the control path
fails cannot be used to compute a prior install-time bound.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{One termination problem for persistent installed state} (\S\ref{sec:model},
\S\ref{sec:problem}). Records and configurations are modelled as state installed while connected
and executed while disconnected, each state's release time is derived from the obligation chain,
and a single local decision problem is stated under four constraints---no-false-release, energy
survival, evidence locality, no-false-completion---with a three-class
confirmed/unconfirmed/unreachable discipline that makes \emph{unknown} a first-class outcome.
\item \textbf{Record termination is standard expiry; cross-segment placement is the gain}
(\S\ref{sec:mech-record}, \S\ref{sec:eval-record}). Deadline-aligned source expiry is
bit-identical to BPv7 per-record lifetime on ten seeds. The system contribution is an end-to-end
business-deadline basis with source/gateway consistency, which doubles outage on-time delivery
and drives expired backup records to one, while gateway-only suppression moves cost to the
access segment. Full-horizon time-aware attribution corrects an earlier accounting that had
labelled 830 late-access failures as backhaul-capacity failures.
\item \textbf{A controlled study of configuration termination}
(\S\ref{sec:mech-lease}, \S\ref{sec:eval-lease}). Preloaded revert bounds eliminate the
\cFiveNightfloorDeadA--\cFiveNightfloorDeadB deaths left by the clock guard. A fixed 8~h TTL
also eliminates those deaths in both phases, while shorter TTLs lose yellow delivery in the
longer phase. An energy-feasibility bound likewise avoids deaths but sacrifices
\cFiveEnergyLostA and \cFiveEnergyLostB yellow deliveries. We identify the information
and installation conditions that a delivery-derived lease must satisfy; its online increment
remains open.
\item \textbf{A formative real-agent study that locates the interface fault}
(\S\ref{sec:eval-agent}). Symmetric accounting of 1089 live decisions shows LoRa access receipts
read as backhaul state in both directions; an unknown-aware projector removes the healthy-link
false assertions in offline replay. The agent is a \emph{controller candidate}, not part of the
problem definition.
\end{enumerate}
We claim no LLM advantage over a solver, no new discard algorithm, and no global scheduling upper
bound. The deterministic mechanisms are in-layer tools available equally to rules, solvers and
agents.

\section{Scenario and Operating Point}
\label{sec:bg}

Fourteen sensor nodes in two groups monitor displacement and rainfall on a slope. Nodes sample
and upload to a local gateway over LoRaWAN Class~A; the gateway forwards to a central server over
cellular backhaul, with a BeiDou short-message service as a sparse, metered backup carrying
field-to-centre messages only, never downlink configuration. Nodes are battery plus small solar,
the mission is pre-disaster and routine with authorised warning-level changes, and no component
judges landslide risk. Node count, role mapping and disturbance parameters follow the frozen Task
v1.1 instance manifest; an earlier 16-node sketch is not used as a field fact.

Authority comes from two Chinese geological-hazard monitoring standards, DZ/T~0460--2023 and
DZ/T~0450--2023, retrieved and clause-audited in the repository~\cite{dzt0460,dzt0450}.
\S5.3.3 permits remote dynamic adjustment of sampling and upload frequency by warning level and
gives no numeric levels, so the periods used here are declared research choices; \S8.4.1 defines
four levels and \S8.4.2 permits upgrade, downgrade or termination after a conference; \S5.3.7
calls for dual-mode communication under weak coverage in high-risk extreme weather---a
conditional clause appearing once, not a blanket dual-channel mandate, and the communication-layer
standard does not impose it. The BeiDou open-service specification
(BDS-OS-PS-3.0)~\cite{bdsps3} reports $\geq$95\% success, sub-2~s average latency, an average
service frequency of one message per 30~s and a 14{,}000-bit single-message ceiling, noting that
realised frequency and maximum length are constrained by registration parameters: the rate is a
provisioned quantity. These clauses bound lawful action---re-compile frequency within granted
levels and request a conference change, never silently drop an obligation or declare risk.

The backup uses one 78-byte packet per 1200~s (20~B header, 58~B payload; a displacement sample is
6~B and rainfall 4~B, so roughly 9--10 samples fit). The elevated (yellow) task uses a 300~s
period with a delivery deadline two periods after window open, a $\approx$600~s delivery
window---shorter than the 1200~s backup slot spacing. The 200~B ceiling and $\geq$1~min period in
DZ/T~0450 (\S7.4.3.2, \S7.4.2.3, with a $\geq$5~s inter-message gap at \S7.4.3.5) belong to
different service-formulation sections and are not combined into one ``BeiDou operating point'';
the 1200~s/78~B point is a declared research choice.

\section{System Model}
\label{sec:model}

Time is slotted at $\Delta{=}60$~s and $t{=}0$ is 06:00 local, so $t{=}12$~h is sunset and the
solar cycle is explicit. Nodes $\mathcal N$, measurands $\mathcal M$ and a gateway $g$ compose the
field; a centre $c$ lies beyond the backhaul.

\subsection{Obligations and the delivery chain}
A monitoring obligation is $o=(n,m,[l_o,h_o),d_o)$ over node $n$, measurand $m$, sampling window
and absolute delivery deadline. A routine obligation at period $P$ has $h_o-l_o=P$ and
$d_o=h_o+P$, one period of grace. A sample fulfils $o$ only if it matches $(n,m)$, is taken
in-window and is received centrally by $d_o$, traversing
\begin{equation}
\resizebox{\columnwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle
\underbrace{\text{qualified sample}}_{\text{node}}
 \!\to\! \underbrace{\text{heard at }g}_{\text{LoRa access}}
 \!\to\! \underbrace{\text{returned by }d_o}_{\text{cellular / short message}}
 \!\to\! \underbrace{\text{central evidence}}_{\text{fulfilled}} .$}
\end{equation}
For piecewise missions the first segment uses a closed interval and later segments half-open
windows, so a boundary sample cannot double-cover adjacent windows after a level change.

\subsection{Links, outages, and who can act}
LoRa access (node$\to$gateway, with Class~A downlink windows) succeeds per opportunity with
probability $p_a{=}0.74$; the primary backhaul (gateway$\to$centre) is good with probability
$p_b{=}0.62$ outside a declared outage. A control outage is an interval $\mathcal O=[a,b)$ during
which the primary is unavailable to centre$\to$field traffic: centre commands and mission-table
segments are refused at admission and never transmitted, and the short-message backup cannot
carry downlink configuration. During $\mathcal O$ the only entities able to change field
communication behaviour are the \emph{node} (on its clock, own battery, cache, recent access
receipts and bounds carried by already-applied commands) and the \emph{gateway} (on samples it
has actually heard, its forwarding log and backup slots used). The centre retains authority to
authorise changes but no channel to install them; a revision authored in $\mathcal O$ reaches the
field at $b$.

\subsection{Persistent installed state}
\emph{Record state.} Each unacknowledged sample $s$ at node $n$ has creation time $t_s$, a
business deadline $d(s)=(\lfloor t_s/P\rfloor+2)P$ under the locally known cadence, and occupies
one of $K{=}240$ cache slots and up to $B{=}32$ positions in an access batch. Under the modelled
edge discipline it leaves the cache only on central acknowledgement (modelled synchronously
without airtime, equally favouring every queue rule); until then it is re-sent oldest-first.

\emph{Configuration state.} The node runs one profile $q(t)\in\{P_0,P_1\}$ (sparse
$P_0{=}600$~s, dense $P_1{=}300$~s) for both sampling and reporting, installed by an applied
command carrying a version. A command is \emph{applied} only when a later node report confirms
the new profile (Class~A latency); queued or in-transit commands change nothing. While applied,
$q{=}P_1$ draws additional sampling and uplink energy every tick until something terminates it.

\subsection{Energy}
A sample costs $e_s{=}4.7{\times}10^{-4}$~Wh and an uplink $e_u{=}2.33{\times}10^{-5}$~Wh;
battery capacity is $C{=}0.05$~Wh. Solar input is a 12~h half-sine with peak $\eta$ (nominally
$0.03$~Wh/h) plus per-node/hour stochastic cloud occlusion. A node whose state of charge reaches
zero \emph{permanently} dies in the main configuration. Holding dense over a 12~h night draws
$\approx0.071$~Wh $>C$.

\subsection{Release time}
For each state $x$, $\trel(x)$ is the earliest time after which keeping $x$ alive can no longer
contribute to a fulfilled obligation. For a record, $\trel(s)=d(s)$: past the business deadline
the record cannot fulfil any obligation, so further retention or retransmission has zero delivery
value. For a configuration profile, $\trel(q)$ is the last deadline of an obligation the
profile's data can still meet through \emph{some} feasible path,
\begin{equation}
\trel(q)=\max_{o:\,\text{profile }q\text{ produces }o}\;
   \min\{\,d(o),\ \tau_{\text{last slot}}(o)\,\},
\label{eq:trelcfg}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_{\text{last slot}}(o)$ is the latest primary or backup return opportunity at which a
qualified sample for $o$ can be heard and forwarded by $d(o)$. Unlike $d(s)$, this bound combines
mission timing---which obligations the current profile serves---with return-slot geometry, when
they can be carried; neither is fully observable at the node during $\mathcal O$.
This is a candidate configuration bound, not a validated online release-time computation.
Stopping new dense acquisition need not stop forwarding already acquired samples; establishing
the earliest safe revert therefore also requires distinguishing these two activities.

\subsection{Service loss and statement discipline}
The objective counts deadline-valid obligation delivery (service), permanent node deaths, and the
resource cost of state outliving its release time: wasted samples, access attempts, short-message
bytes and energy. Offline, each unfulfilled obligation is charged to its \emph{first} failing
stage under an optimistic model granting every later assumption, giving four mutually exclusive
segments: \senergy\ (no qualified sample), \saccess\ (sampled but not heard at the gateway by the
deadline), \stime\ (heard but no return opportunity exists) and \scap\ (an opportunity exists but
capacity or congestion cannot carry it). An online observer that cannot see a stage reports
\emph{unknown} and never guesses it. Every feasibility claim is \textbf{confirmed} (an independent
report shows the target config, or a matching sample is received centrally),
\textbf{unconfirmed} (a command is in flight or receipts are incomplete, which implies neither
that it took effect nor that the channel is down) or \textbf{unreachable} (the current path is
known down from explicit network-side state). A LoRa access receipt---``a node was heard''---is
evidence about the access link only and never by itself establishes backhaul reachability.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

During an outage $\mathcal O$, at each tick $t$ each acting entity chooses, for the state it can
touch, an action: for a record $a_t(s)\in\{\textsc{retain},\textsc{release}\}$; for the
configuration $a_t(q)\in\{\textsc{hold},\textsc{revert}\}$, reverting dense to the last confirmed
sparse profile. Let $\mathcal E_e(t)$ be the evidence entity $e$ lawfully holds at $t$---for a
node, clock, own SoC, cache contents, recent Class~A receipts and any bound carried by commands
applied before $a$, and explicitly \emph{not} future mission-table segments, simulator-wide
samples or backhaul truth. The local termination problem is
{\small
\begin{align}
\textbf{Problem 1.}\quad \min_{\{a_t\}}\quad
 & \textstyle\sum_o \mathbf 1[o\ \text{unfulfilled}]\notag\\
 & \textstyle{}+\lambda_d\sum_n \mathbf 1[n\ \text{dies}]\notag\\
 & \textstyle{}+\lambda_w\!\!\sum_{x:\,t\geq\trel(x)}\!\! \operatorname{cost}(x\ \text{kept}) \label{eq:obj}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
 & \text{(C1)}\ \textsc{release/revert}\Rightarrow t\geq\trel(x), \label{eq:c1}\\
 & \text{(C2)}\ \operatorname{SoC}_n(t)>0\ \ \forall n,t, \label{eq:c2}\\
 & \text{(C3)}\ a_t=f(\mathcal E_e(t)), \label{eq:c3}\\
 & \text{(C4)}\ \text{unknown}\Rightarrow\text{unconfirmed/unfulfillable},\label{eq:c4}
\end{align}}
where (C1) is no false release, (C2) energy survival, (C3) evidence locality and (C4) no false
completion, which forbids reporting either fulfilment or unreachability under unknown feasibility.
The tension is between \eqref{eq:c1} and \eqref{eq:c2} under locality \eqref{eq:c3}. Releasing too
early violates \eqref{eq:c1}, abandoning work that could still be delivered; releasing too late
violates \eqref{eq:c2}, paying for dense data after they became undeliverable and discharging
fatally. The difficulty is that the two state types observe $\trel$ very differently.

\subsection{Record sub-problem: $\trel$ is locally certain}
Because $d(s)$ uses only the public cadence, the node evaluates $\trel(s)=d(s)$ with certainty and
no downlink. Constraint~\eqref{eq:c1} is the exact test $d(s)\le t$, and \textsc{release} frees
both the backup send and the access batch. The optimal local rule is ordinary per-record expiry;
\S\ref{sec:mech-record} shows a certificate-keyed variant is the same rule, and that the only
design choice with measurable consequence is \emph{where the release is executed}, through
\eqref{eq:c3} and the cross-segment acknowledgement coupling.

\subsection{Configuration sub-problem: $\trel$ is not locally observable}
During $\mathcal O$, future mission revisions cannot be inferred from missing messages.
A clock guard, a fixed-duration lease and a node-local SoC guard use different locally available
signals. Their feasibility must be compared under the same prior mission knowledge and energy
conditions. The present experiments evaluate the clock guard and preloaded revert times; they
do not evaluate a node-local SoC guard in the same configuration-termination comparison.

\textbf{Proposed delivery-bounded lease.} If a mission's validity end and relevant return
opportunities are already authorised and known while connected, a gateway could derive an
absolute bound and transmit it with the configuration. A later revision would require a new
version and lease. If the downgrade is first authorised during the outage, its time is unavailable
for this calculation. The current prototype instead preloads a bound computed from the full
experimental schedule. It demonstrates the effect of local termination but does not implement
command-carried, version-bound lease installation or prove an online solution to
\eqref{eq:c1}--\eqref{eq:c3}. An ordinary mission-validity expiry with the same information is
also required as a comparator.

\subsection{Why this is a communication problem}
Problem~1 is defined over network state, evidence locality and return geometry; it has an optimal
oracle solution and ordinary rule-based solutions independent of any LLM. An agent is one possible
\emph{solver} of the same decision and a convenient probe of where the interface misleads a
planner; nothing in the objective or constraints references a model. We therefore evaluate the
mechanisms deterministically and treat the LLM study as formative.

\section{Mechanisms}
\label{sec:mech}

\subsection{Record termination: deadline-aligned expiry}
\label{sec:mech-record}
At each upload opportunity the node deletes cached records with $d(s)\le t$ and sends survivors
oldest-first. We implement two formulations: \texttt{deadline-purge}, keyed to the obligation
cadence, and \texttt{generic-expiry}, an ordinary BPv7-style per-record lifetime assigned at
creation, $\text{expires}=t_s+((P-t_s\bmod P)+P)=d(s)$, referencing no obligation id, slot
geometry or certificate. A gateway variant locally acknowledges records it will not send. EDF and
obligation-greedy queues only reorder and never release; AoI \texttt{latest-only} releases
indiscriminately, keeping only the newest. Dropping stale or expired traffic is well established
in AoI packet management and DTN bundle expiry~\cite{costa2014pm,kam2016deadline,dtnarch,rfc9171},
and suppressing sensor traffic whose delay bound is exceeded is likewise prior
art~\cite{wada2009timeout}. We claim no new discard algorithm. The system-level content is the
end-to-end business-deadline basis---not packet age, hop TTL or an estimated single-packet
delay---zero-downlink source placement, and, decisively, cross-segment consistency, because the
acknowledgement coupling means a gateway-only release does not free the source cache.

\subsection{Configuration termination: guard and delivery-bounded lease}
\label{sec:mech-lease}
The implemented \emph{local clock guard} clamps sampling and reporting to sparse at night
(hod $<06{:}00$ or $\geq18{:}00$) and leaves daytime commands untouched. It is off by default
and bit-identical when off. A node-local SoC guard is a distinct ordinary comparator, not part
of this clock-only implementation.

The configuration experiment adds an absolute revert time to each node's local guard at
initialisation. At or after that time the node clamps a dense profile back to sparse without a
downlink. This is a preloaded bound experiment: the bound is not received through the configuration
command, renewed by its version, or computed from an online gateway evidence view.
The earlier delivery-derived setting used the full schedule's downgrade time and rounded the
last yellow deadline \emph{up} to the next backup slot; this can lie after the deadline, and thus
is not the latest feasible slot defined in Eq.~\ref{eq:trelcfg}. That construction also predicted
feasibility with a ledger that omitted the battery-capacity term, and its reading of ``zero deaths
with no loss of yellow delivery'' was retracted: under the corrected ledger the energy-feasibility
gates keep zero deaths but give up \cFiveEnergyLostA and \cFiveEnergyLostB deliveries
(\ref{tab:lease}). The runs are retained as timing interventions, and the construction is not
claimed to implement Eq.~\ref{eq:trelcfg}.

\subsection{Partial-observability projector}
\label{sec:mech-projector}
At compile time the centre holds the obligation table, the \emph{current} and never forecast
network-side outage state, and public parameters; at mission-table arrival the gateway holds only
what it has heard. A structural no-return certificate (\stime) is emitted from slot geometry alone
and is explicitly conditional on the primary being unavailable; geometry never infers an outage.
An arrival-time projector labels a missed obligation only when local evidence separates the
stages, and otherwise returns unknown. An unknown-aware centre projector emits \emph{unreachable}
solely from an explicit network-side field---which the current harness does not yet provide, so it
emits \emph{unconfirmed}---and replaces any blanket night kill with the computed energy ledger, so
genuine energy-infeasibility is distinguished from conservative advice.

\section{Prototype and Methodology}
\label{sec:proto}

All mechanisms run in one Python event-stepped simulator (60~s ticks) implementing LoRaWAN Class~A
downlink windows, versioned two-field configuration commands (sampling interval and reporting
period delivered as independent fields, with cross-generation mixed configs detected), the
hold-until-acknowledged edge cache, max-coverage backup packing, stochastic solar and cloud,
absorbing brownout, and the non-prescient piecewise mission-view gate. Disabling the backup
reproduces the v1.1 simulator bit-for-bit across five anchored invariants; the local guard and
lease are default-off.

Two controlled episodes are used. \emph{Episode~I}, the v1.1 manifest point, upgrades to yellow at
$t{=}6$~h with primary outage 4--20~h and the mission table reaching the gateway at 20~h; it drives
the frontier, attribution, record-expiry, placement and live-agent results. \emph{Episode~II} is a
deterministic configuration-termination scenario with identical parameters except schedule,
outage phasing and swept harvest: to place a daytime downgrade \emph{inside} an outage, phase~A
uses yellow at $t{=}2$~h with a conferred downgrade at $t{=}6$~h and outage 4--20~h, phase~B uses
yellow at $t{=}1$~h, downgrade at $t{=}8$~h and outage 6--22~h; $t{=}12$~h is sunset. Episode~II is
an explicitly constructed controlled comparison for the configuration sub-problem, not a new field
claim. Node deaths, deterministic and highly robust, are reported as totals over seeds 0--2;
sub-point mean-service differences at $n{=}3$ are treated as not significant; seeds 6--9 are a
reused held-out confirmation set, not an untouched test set. Every figure is regenerated by a
registered repository script and the suite reports 20/20 self-checks.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Where the fixed-resource loss is}
\label{sec:eval-frontier}
Over Episode~I's 7560 routine obligations the strong maxcov/FIFO baseline delivers 3025 (0.400).
Symmetric per-obligation counterfactuals widen one physical resource at a time, never the policy,
and report set differences over identical obligation ids. Relaxing backhaul to unlimited gives
4740 (0.627), relaxing energy (0.50~Wh, always-dense) gives 4089 (0.541), and both gives 6023
(0.797); 1715 obligations are blocked by backhaul capacity alone, 1128 by battery alone, and 155
(2.0\%) by both (Table~\ref{tab:walls}). These are intervention results on implemented strategies,
not an exhaustive optimum; we make no zero-remainder or global-optimality claim. They establish
that the large throughput gains are physical, which is why we then target \emph{state termination}
rather than a smarter scheduler.

\subsection{Record expiry: standard primitive, decisive placement}
\label{sec:eval-record}
During the Episode~I outage 75 backup packets carry 750 records; under FIFO 219 arrive centrally
already expired against 261 on-time (45.6\%; 1270 of 2782 payload bytes). Gateway-only suppression
removes those sends but withholds the acknowledgement, and on-time delivery falls $202\to194$: cost
moves to the access segment. Source-local deadline expiry reverses the sign, raising outage on-time
delivery $202\to434$ and the deadline-valid fraction $0.54\to1.00$. Across ten seeds
(Table~\ref{tab:purgeexpiry}) expiry raises mean service $0.373\to0.415$, a paired $+4.21$ points
(95\% CI $+3.64$ to $+4.79$, positive on all ten), total outage on-time delivery $1691\to4265$
(2.52$\times$), expired backup records $2538\to1$, and deaths $12\to0$; it beats AoI latest-only by
$+5.73$ points (CI $+5.06$ to $+6.39$), because latest-only buys outage freshness by abandoning
post-recovery coverage. Crucially, \texttt{deadline-purge} and \texttt{generic-expiry} are
bit-identical on every seed---service, outage counts, deaths and delivered set---so the record
mechanism \emph{is} standard per-record expiration, and its measured value is the business-deadline
basis plus source/gateway cross-segment consistency, not a new discard algorithm.

\subsection{Configuration leases: deaths under overcast skies, false release under short TTL}

new dense acquisition from retaining samples that can still be forwarded, and report the lost

\subsection{What the field can attribute when the table arrives}
\label{sec:eval-attr}
In Episode~I the delayed mission table reaches the gateway at $t_a{=}20$~h, by which point 2136
yellow obligations are past deadline. Offline time-aware attribution labels all 2136 correctly
(\stime\ 588, \scap\ 327, \saccess\ 650, \senergy\ 571). Using only evidence the gateway lawfully
holds by $t_a$ labels 1138/2136 (53.3\%)---all geometric \stime\ (588), all in-time-not-returned
\scap\ (327) and 223 provably late \saccess---at 100\% precision on the labelled subset; 998
(46.7\%) remain unknown, whose offline truth is 571 never-sampled and 427 heard only after $t_a$
(Table~\ref{tab:attribution}). ``Never sampled'' and ``sampled but not yet heard'' are inseparable
without evidence the gateway does not have, and must stay unknown, which is the original G5
constraint. Compile-time geometry, needing no sample, emits 602 conditional no-return certificates
of which 588 are true \stime, the 14 recovery-edge cases being excluded rather than certified; an
optimistic-energy capacity certificate is impure (552 correct \scap, 571 actually \senergy, 193
false positives), so capacity is left to the gateway. The design consequence is exactly Problem~1's:
local record and configuration action must not wait on resolving these unknowns.

\subsection{Enforcement placement determines survival}
\label{sec:eval-place}
A centre-side feasibility \emph{envelope}---a pure filter over any planner using only confirmed
configs, hearing counts, the authorised requirement and the clock---removes the command storm but
cannot save the tightest seed, because a dusk downgrade must cross the backhaul and a Class~A
window it misses. The same ``never stay dense overnight'' rule is a late, refusable suggestion at
the centre and an invariant enforced every tick at the node (Table~\ref{tab:r39}). Naive centre
compliance kills 13 of 14 nodes on all three seeds (39 total) at service 0.162; adding the
node-local guard removes all deaths at unchanged service; with the guard present the envelope
additionally cuts refused admission attempts to 222. A zero-command pre-provisioned rhythm reaches
0.360 service, matching every centre-compiled arm for these static missions---a scoped negative
result on centre sampling control for pre-provisioned tasks, which is exactly the gap the delivery
lease targets for \emph{non}-pre-provisioned runtime changes.

\subsection{Formative real-agent study}
\label{sec:eval-agent}
We ran deepseek-flash live over the Episode~I outage: three seeds each of an unaided agent (A0), the
agent with an ordinary pending-command checklist (A0s) and a certificate agent (A1), plus two
healthy-link controls, with a real model call at each 30-minute decision, 99 per trace and 1089
decisions in total; all arms share harness, legal observation, tools, history and action space.
The study motivates and stress-tests the interface and is not used to establish the deterministic
mechanisms.

\emph{Service and survival, noisy at $n{=}3$.} A1 attains mean service 0.358, level with the 0.360
deterministic safe floor, with 4 deaths; A0 0.296 with 35 deaths and A0s 0.222 with 39; the
healthy-link controls give A0 0.275/13 and A1 0.511/0. Per-seed service overlaps, so we draw no
service significance. The live agent loop was not wired to the local guard, so the four A1 seed-1
deaths are not cleared by the deterministic result of \S\ref{sec:eval-place}.

\emph{Call accounting.} The 1089 decisions are not 1089 successful calls: 52 ended in a
parse-failure hold from \texttt{max\_tokens} cutting the JSON mid-string, and 412 records carry a
first-attempt error even when the retry succeeded, a logging artefact. Per-arm parse-failure rates
are A0 7.1\% (28/396), A0s 6.4\% (19/297) and A1 1.3\% (5/396), so arm differences mix
output-format reliability with any semantic effect.

\emph{The interface fault, symmetrically scored.} The observation's \texttt{link} field carries
only LoRa access receipts (\texttt{n\_heard}) and no backhaul state. In the forced outage window
A0/A0s assert ``link up, commands can apply'' on 36/32 decisions, and A1 on 1 after token-boundary
de-noising of an ``uplink-only'' substring---deterministically over-optimistic, since the primary
is down throughout, and accompanying the ineffective dense orders and deaths. In the opposite
direction the v4 certificate asserted ``control plane not delivering'' whenever fewer than all
nodes were heard, firing at five healthy-link times when the primary was in fact usable, twice
after 13 of 14 nodes had already confirmed dense. The earlier false-report tally was invalid on
three counts: it ran only on decisions that ordered dense, vacuous for the mostly-hold A1; it never
ran on the healthy group, so those zeros were initialisation; and it counted keywords the
certificate prompt itself taught, terms appearing in 317 A1 decisions and zero A0/A0s decisions.
Offline replay of an unknown-aware v5 projector on identical snapshots issues zero unreachable
assertions without a network-side field, turning all five healthy false alarms into unconfirmed,
and splits 528 blanket night kills into 189 genuinely energy-infeasible and 339 advisory, every
near-dawn 04:30--06:00 case being advisory. The honest finding is that a missing backhaul-state
signal causes bidirectional errors; isolating any independent increment of the projector requires a
matched-capability arm receiving the same constants and heuristics without the projector,
end-to-end v5 runs with an explicit backhaul-state field, and cross-model replication, all future
work.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{LLMs in emergency and network control.} TopoLLM applies adaptive tool learning to
post-disaster topology planning~\cite{topollm}, and later systems let LLMs schedule UAV paths and
bandwidth, shape RL rewards or orchestrate recovery tools, including intent objects, budget and
time constraints, candidate-plan checks, local repair and documented network-management
APIs~\cite{icgrestore,ossgpt}. We claim no novelty for natural language to constraints, nor for
tool orchestration. These decide network resources or compose recovery tools under an assumed
reachable control path; they do not terminate state that must keep acting after that path dies, and
do not maintain an unknown-aware three-class state over a long routine monitoring workflow. Agentic
Wi-Fi likewise uses an LLM as a solver for a separately defined coordination protocol; we follow
that ordering---problem first, agent optional.

\textbf{Scheduling, freshness, energy.} The closest state-model precedent is an LLM in-context
data-collection scheduler with bounded per-sensor queues, exogenous arrivals, finite service
opportunities and feedback~\cite{icldc}; AoI and energy-harvesting control, including
instantaneous-battery threshold policies and partially observed harvesting, address resource
pieces~\cite{bacinoglu1905,zakeri2311,costa2014pm}; overbooking and contact-aware forwarding for
space links~\cite{ccsdssabr} and network-calculus bounds~\cite{leboudec01} are complementary
deterministic characterisations. Joint sampling, transmission and energy optimisation is
established and not claimed here; our distinction is persistent \emph{remote} configuration with
Class~A apply latency, a separated access versus primary/backup return path, and termination under a
control outage. Consistent network update research has long studied per-update validity,
dependencies and safe transitions~\cite{sdnconsistency}; we rely on that lesson and add the
disconnected, energy-constrained edge case in which an installed update's \emph{own data} become
undeliverable before a counter-update can arrive.

\textbf{Packet discard, DTN expiry, contacts.} AoI management pre-empts or drops old
updates~\cite{kam2016deadline,krishnan2019multihop}; deadline-aware buffer discard has been coupled
with adaptive ARQ for video~\cite{razavivideo}; DTN bundle protocols attach creation-time lifetimes
and use TTL or encounter drop~\cite{dtnarch,iranmaneshdtnqueue,rfc9171}, and custody
transfer~\cite{rfc5050} moves retention responsibility as a forward hand-off, since migrated out of
the BPv7 core. Rural LoRa--LEO systems schedule around estimated satellite contacts and exploit
spatial gateway diversity~\cite{sateriot}. Our record disposition \emph{is} BPv7-style expiry and we
demonstrate equivalence on the tested seeds; the proposed configuration lease reuses the
TTL/lease primitive with a delivery-derived bound. The claimed deltas are narrow and explicit: an end-to-end business-deadline basis with
cross-segment source/gateway consistency, since expired backhaul suppression alone back-pressures
access, and the measured effect of preloaded configuration termination. Deriving and installing such a
bound online, with an increment over equally informed ordinary leases, remains open.

\section{Discussion, Limitations, and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:disc}

\textbf{Scope.} The throughput negative result and the resource walls are scoped to the policy
families and one operating point; they are not an optimum, and a better scheduler could exist. The
configuration results cover two constructed phases, with five harvest levels in phase~A and
two in phase~B. They show that earlier local reversion can avoid deaths left by a clock guard;
a fixed 8~h TTL also succeeds in both phases by the reported death and yellow-delivery counts.
The candidate bound uses the future downgrade time and is preloaded. Its derivation under
install-time knowledge, command delivery, renewal and version semantics remain unvalidated.
Future-authorised revisions must not enter the calculation. Wider physical sweeps can test
robustness after this information boundary is enforced.

\textbf{Model simplifications.} Central acknowledgement is synchronous and airtime-free, equally
favouring every queue rule; gateway acknowledgements would consume Class~A windows in deployment.
During Episode~I the node tests deadlines against the public pre-change cadence, with post-upgrade
deadlines used only in offline accounting. Single fixed-period missions make obligation-keyed and
deadline-keyed record release coincide; multi-obligation or overlapping mission versions require
release keyed to a record's latest still-valid deadline. Episode~II is a controlled scenario for the
configuration sub-problem, not an independent field measurement.

\textbf{Agent evidence.} The live study is single-model, three seeds per arm, temperature 0, with
large per-run variance and a confounded expert packet; it demonstrates the interface fault and the
projector's offline effect, not an independent agent increment. The hardened harness---per-request
accounting, symmetric scoring, unknown-aware projector and energy ledger, default-off local guard
and lease---is in the repository so that matched-capability, end-to-end v5 and cross-model studies
can run without re-litigating instrumentation; those are explicitly not claimed here.

\textbf{Provenance.} One standards channel was obtained from a document-sharing host, a top-grade
document with a lower-grade copy chain, because the official host was unreachable by TLS; clause
numbers were cross-checked from two independent copies, and unverified international
LoRa-plus-satellite and vendor lines are not used as fact. We did not execute cited systems'
implementations and claim no replication of them.

\section{Conclusion}
After a backhaul failure the communication state installed beforehand keeps executing, and its
termination cannot wait for the control path that failed. We formulated this as one local
termination problem over two kinds of persistent installed state. Records carry a locally certain
release time, the business deadline, and standard per-record expiry placed consistently at the
source doubles outage on-time delivery while gateway-only suppression merely moves cost to the
access segment. Configuration experiments show that preloaded local reversion can prevent deaths left by
sunset-only control, with fixed TTL 8~h also succeeding in both tested phases. A delivery-derived
lease remains a candidate whose online knowledge and installation semantics require validation.
The evidence discipline applies throughout: an observer must distinguish confirmed outcomes from
unknown state. An LLM is one candidate controller; the formative live study exposes access
receipts being mistaken for backhaul state.
