## PART 2 — POSITIONING

### A. Tool / execution reliability and execution semantics

**1. NetArena: Dynamic Benchmarks for AI Agents in Network Automation**
- Venue/year: **ICLR 2026** (poster); arXiv preprint 2506.03231 (2025).
- What it does: dynamically generates executable network-automation tasks/environments for LLM agents, usable as an RL training environment.
- Tool/execution reliability: **YES** — executable environment, closed-loop evaluation.
- Sources: <https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10010955>, <https://openreview.net/pdf?id=BPVPOtzoOz>, <https://github.com/Froot-NetSys/NetArena>

**2. NetConfArena: An Executable Benchmark for LLM Agents in Closed-Loop Network Configuration**
- Venue/year: **arXiv preprint 2608.23179**, submitted 24 Aug 2026 (venue unknown).
- What it does: places agents in emulated multi-device networks behind a standardized compact action interface, to expose reliability and failure patterns in closed-loop configuration; argues prior benchmarks understate protocol-complexity and topology-dependence.
- Tool/execution reliability: **YES, explicitly** — the paper's stated motivation is that "their reliability and failure patterns are poorly understood."
- Source: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23179>

**3. WirelessBench: A Tolerance-Aware LLM Agent Benchmark for Wireless Network Intelligence**
- Venue/year: **arXiv preprint 2603.21251**, submitted 22 Mar 2026. Author comment on arXiv: "This paper is submitted to a possiable [sic] journal for publication" — **venue unverified**.
- What it does: a tolerance-aware, tool-integrated benchmark for LLM wireless agents, in three tiers — domain knowledge reasoning (WCHW, 1,392 items), intent-driven resource allocation (WCNS, 1,000 items), proactive multi-step decisions under mobility (WCMSA, 1,000 items). Targets cascaded-chain failures and catastrophic unit confusions (dB vs dBm).
- Tool/execution reliability: **YES** — this is the closest existing relative to the proposed paper. Its explicit gap claim is that existing wireless benchmarks "evaluate single isolated capabilities and treat all errors uniformly."
- Source: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21251>

**4. FaulT-Bench: Towards Benchmarking Network Troubleshooting LLM Agents under Unreliable User Tickets**
- Venue/year: **arXiv preprint 2608.27021**, submitted 27 Aug 2026 (venue unknown).
- What it does: 200 troubleshooting scenarios across eight topologies (five reimplemented from public practitioner labs) covering genuine faults, false fault reports, incorrect device attribution, and incorrect root-cause claims; rewrites 72 false-premise tickets into five reporter personas; an **automated harness deploys each scenario**.
- Tool/execution reliability: **PARTIAL** — it stress-tests *input* reliability and runs a real execution harness, but its object of study is ticket quality, not tool-invocation semantics.
- Source: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27021>

**5. Beyond State Machines: Executing Network Procedures with Agentic Tool-Calling Sequences**
- Venue/year: **arXiv preprint 2605.02584**, submitted 4 May 2026 (cs.NI + cs.AI). Venue unknown.
- What it does: compares four approaches that differ in how the agent obtains a network procedure and in how execution is split between the agent and the underlying tools, measuring **latency and execution correctness**, with a stress test on long sequential tool-call chains; case study is a UE IP-allocation procedure.
- Tool/execution reliability: **YES — this is the clearest execution-semantics paper I found.** It evaluates *where* execution lives (agent vs. tool) rather than only plan quality. But it has no wireless/emergency framing.
- Source: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02584>

### B. Network self-healing / autonomous RAN / intent-based management

**6. ORION: Intent-Aware Orchestration in Open RAN for SLA-Driven Network Management**
- Venue/year: **arXiv preprint 2603.03667**, submitted 4 Mar 2026.
- What it does: O-RAN-compliant intent orchestration using LLMs via the **Model Context Protocol (MCP)** in a hierarchical agent architecture, translating natural-language intents into enforceable network policies with end-to-end intent assurance.
- Tool/execution reliability: **NO** — planning/translation quality. MCP tool use is the mechanism, not the object of evaluation.
- Source: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03667>

**7. Agentic AI-RAN: Enabling Intent-Driven, Explainable and Self-Evolving Open RAN Intelligence**
- Venue/year: **IEEE Network Magazine (accepted)**, arXiv 2602.24115, v1 27 Feb 2026 / v2 3 Jul 2026.
- What it does: survey/positioning piece on bringing agentic controllers (explicit planning, tool use, memory, self-management) into O-RAN's Non-RT/Near-RT RIC and DU surfaces; organises tasks into network slice life-cycle, radio resource management, and related clusters; motivates safe and auditable multi-tenant operation.
- Tool/execution reliability: **NO** — architecture/planning survey. "Safe and auditable" is motivation, not a measured result.
- Sources: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24115>, <https://xplorestaging.ieee.org/document/11613135>

**8. Advanced AI Service Provisioning in O-RAN through LLM Engine Integration**
- Venue/year: **arXiv preprint 2605.23809**, v1 22 May 2026 / v2 3 Jun 2026 (proof-of-concept).
- What it does: a "Dual-Brain" architecture — an LLM orchestrator translates operator intents into data-collection policies and deployment code, while an automated ML engine (NeuralSmith) trains lightweight classifiers on demand.
- Tool/execution reliability: **PARTIAL / NO benchmark.** The paper *asserts* the core reliability tension — LLMs are "unsuited for the fast, deterministic inference required in real-time RAN control" — and routes around it architecturally, but does not measure execution reliability.
- Source: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23809>

**9. In-Context Autonomous Network Incident Response: An End-to-End Large Language Model Agent Approach**
- Venue/year: **2026 AAAI Summer Symposium on Human-Aware AI Agents for the Cyber Battlefield**; arXiv 2602.13156, v1 13 Feb 2026 / v2 15 Apr 2026.
- What it does: end-to-end agentic incident-response *planning* from raw system logs and alerts, using an agent with four integrated functional components; explicitly positioned against RL approaches that need handcrafted simulators and discard raw-log semantics.
- Tool/execution reliability: **NO** — response planning quality. Incident response is the self-healing analogue, but there is no execution-runtime analysis.
- Sources: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13156>, <https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI-SS/article/view/42938>

**10. LLM-Powered Agentic AI for 5G/6G Networks: A Tutorial and Survey on Architectures, Protocols, and Standardization**
- Venue/year: **arXiv preprint 2607.16066**, 17 Jul 2026, 35 pages, "Under review". Venue unknown.
- What it does: two-part tutorial-and-survey. Part I formalises 5G/6G control, management and AI-native planes and covers agentic foundations — reasoning, planning, **tool use**, multi-agent coordination, and **evaluation**; Part II maps agentic capabilities onto control surfaces, standardisation and 6G initiatives; closes with open challenges.
- Tool/execution reliability: **SURVEY** — names tool use and evaluation as covered foundations. Useful as a citation anchor and as evidence that the community regards agent evaluation as underdeveloped relative to planning.
- Source: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16066>

## Which venue family actually publishes at this intersection?

**Honest answer: today, no single family does. The two halves live in different communities, and that split is the opportunity.**

Where the *wireless/emergency + LLM-agent* work lands — communications venues and journals:
- IEEE TMC (MRLMN, 2026) · IEEE Network Magazine (Agentic AI-RAN) · Physical Communication/Elsevier (UAV emergency DRL) · CogSIMA 2026 (GenAI emergency agents) · AMCIS 2026 (crisis informatics) · ESA 4S Symposium 2026 (onboard EO agents) · AAAI Summer Symposium 2026 (incident response).

Where the *agent execution-reliability / runtime* work lands — AI and systems/measurement venues:
- ICLR 2026 (NetArena) · arXiv preprints aimed at systems, networking-measurement and AI venues (NetConfArena, FaulT-Bench, Beyond State Machines, WirelessBench, AERIS). **None of these is in a communications venue.**

The nearest approaches to a true intersection are (a) **WirelessBench** — wireless-domain, tool-integrated, error-taxonomy-aware, but a benchmark only and venue-unknown; (b) **Beyond State Machines** — execution semantics of tool-calling, but generic telco procedure, no wireless/emergency framing and no runtime artifact; and (c) **AERIS** — a real runtime with execution semantics, but aerial-edge robotics, not emergency communications.

**I found no paper that is simultaneously (1) an execution-semantics benchmark, (2) a runtime system, and (3) framed around wireless/emergency communication.**

Two signals that make the systems route viable right now:
- **SenSys 2027 explicitly lists benchmarks/tools/frameworks as a first-class submission category**, alongside LLMs for edge/embedded systems, fault tolerance/dependability/robustness, and systems for extreme environments.
- **INFOCOM 2027 lists "Agentic communication and networking" as a topic** — the strongest signal on the communications side that agentic work is now in scope, paired with "Fault tolerance, reliability, and survivability."

## Recommendation

**Primary target: ACM SenSys 2027, second deadline — abstract 29 Oct 2026, full paper 5 Nov 2026.** Submit under the **Benchmarks / Tools** category: full paper (12 pp) if you have a real emergency-comms evaluation; short paper (6 pp) if the core contribution is the execution-semantics benchmark plus a demonstrative runtime, since short papers are explicitly "evaluated on originality, clarity, and potential impact, even without extensive evaluation." That is ~8 weeks out, enough time to build and freeze the artifact.

Reasoning:
1. **Contribution type matches the venue, not just the topic.** An execution-semantics benchmark + runtime is a *systems* claim — "does the runtime do what it says under failure injection?" — evaluated by reproducibility and artifact criteria. SenSys names that category explicitly, and offers a 6-page short format so you don't have to inflate a benchmark into a 12-page systems paper.
2. **The emergency setting is in scope** via "systems for extreme environments", "fault-tolerance, dependability, and robustness", and satellite/UAV entries. You do not need to argue wireless relevance into a non-wireless venue — SenSys is the merged ACM/IEEE sensing + embedded-AI venue and already covers wireless communication.
3. **Two-deadline model is a real hedge.** If the Nov 2026 submission gets borderline reviews, the resubmission path is defined (substantive revision + ≤4-page "Response to Reviewers") — and you get the reviews either way.
4. **Caveat to respect:** SenSys 2027 declares survey/tutorial papers out of scope. This must be a benchmark + runtime with real measurements and failure analysis, not a taxonomy paper.

**Highest-value stretch, only if the artifact is ready now: NSDI '27 fall, 17 Sep 2026.** Higher prestige, real artifact evaluation, and a Frontiers Track built for "bold ideas … challenging to evaluate in the traditional sense." But the title/abstract registration was **10 Sep 2026** (effectively now) and the full paper is 5 days out — realistic only if the artifact already exists and was registered.

**Safe, high-fit fallback: IEEE TNSM (rolling, no deadline).** Its editorial stance explicitly welcomes "applied contributions (reporting on experiences and experiments with actual systems)", it gives 10 free pages *including* references, and it runs to 16 pages — enough for a benchmark plus runtime plus a full emergency-comms case study without cramming. **IEEE TMC (rolling)** is the second fallback and carries direct precedent: TMC published MRLMN, an LLM-driven UAV emergency-communication networking paper, in 2026, so editorial appetite is demonstrable.

**Why a communications conference is the weaker primary fit despite the topical match:**
- **Page limits work against you.** ICC 2027 (2 Oct 2026) and WCNC 2027 (15 Sep 2026) are hard **6-page** limits — verified for the ComSoc family via GLOBECOM 2026 and WCNC 2027 submission guidelines. A benchmark + runtime + evaluation does not fit in 6 pages, and reviews at those venues reward a new communication mechanism or optimisation result, not a failure taxonomy. Use them only for a compact "first results" version.
- **INFOCOM's own cycle is closed.** INFOCOM 2027 passed on 31 Jul 2026; the next is ~July 2027. Even then, the 9-page main-text limit is tight for a benchmark, and INFOCOM has no artifact-evaluation track. If you go the INFOCOM route, plan it as a *follow-up* that uses your benchmark to demonstrate a specific emergency-network mechanism — where "Agentic communication and networking" and "Fault tolerance, reliability, and survivability" give you a topical home.
- **MobiCom/MobiSys** are credible systems venues but weaker fits here: MobiSys 2027 has no published dates (all "TBA", expected ~Dec 2026) and no advertised benchmarks category; MobiCom 2027's next round is the winter deadline, expected ~Mar 2027 and also TBD.
- **CoNEXT is out**, by its own scope rule: wireless-PHY-focused work without demonstrated network-level impact is explicitly out of scope, and PACMNET would judge an execution-semantics benchmark on networking contribution.
- **SIGCOMM is out** culturally for this contribution type (and its CFP is TBA, ~Feb 2027).
- **COMPASS is out** unless you reframe around marginalized communities and sustainable-society impact; its deadline is ~Mar 2027 and its review culture is interdisciplinary, not systems.
- **IPSN no longer exists as a separate venue** — submit to SenSys.

**Suggested plan:** SenSys 2027 (Nov 5, 2026) as primary → if rejected, IEEE TNSM immediately (rolling, extended version with the emergency-comms case study) → and in parallel aim the mechanism follow-up at INFOCOM 2028 (~Jul 2027) under the "Agentic communication and networking" topic.
