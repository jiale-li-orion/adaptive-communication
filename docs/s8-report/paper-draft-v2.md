## Abstract

Pre-disaster geohazard monitoring must keep satisfying periodic monitoring *obligations* when the cellular backhaul is out for hours and a narrow short-message service (e.g., RDSS-style satellite packets) is the only way out, with small solar-powered nodes behind a LoRaWAN Class A access segment. We separate two decision levels that prior work tends to conflate. **(I) Upstream configuration**—how densely to sample and how often to report—has, in this setting, almost no *dynamic* headroom: across paired seeds the strongest feasible static operating point (sample/report matched to the obligation period) reaches 0.984 timely coverage and physically dominates every adaptive rule we could field, including an ordinary rolling MPC (0.647) and an obligation-driven joint policy (0.937, high variance, ≈80× the commands, whose sampling and report-only ablations are identical). **(II) Backup content arbitration**—which already-collected samples occupy each sparse backup packet—is where adaptation matters, and only under scarcity. Scarcity has **two axes**, capacity (net throughput vs. obligation production) and opportunity spacing; in particular a regime can have nominal per-hour capacity above production yet fail because packets are far apart. Under capacity scarcity (0.46–0.69 of production) or opportunity sparsity, classical item-level rules (FIFO/EDF/newest-first, obligation de-duplication, expiry-salvage) stall between 0.46 and 0.84, and no single item-level rule wins across regimes. We show why (aggressive per-obligation de-duplication stalls packet emission and builds gateway backlog up to 41,400 s; expiry-salvage drops samples the center still counts) and then apply the textbook strong rule this setting calls for—a greedy **marginal-coverage-with-recency** packer (`maxcov`): within each packet it repeatedly takes the sample with the largest *marginal newly covered, still-salvageable obligations per byte*, deadline then recency breaking ties, and fills residual bytes newest-first, **never skipping a send opportunity**. At the **same backup bytes**, maxcov reaches 0.929/0.773 in the two capacity-scarce regimes, +9.1/+11.7 pp over the strongest item-level rule, while a no-redundant-filler variant reaches 0.953 in the sparse-large-packet regime; in saturation item-level EDF/newest-first already reach 0.984 and maxcov trails by 2.4 pp, a reported boundary rather than an engineered-away residual. Repaired ablations (with branch witnesses and stable-id pairing) attribute the gain to obligation-level marginal coverage, in-packet marginal recomputation, and non-stalling—not to cross-packet global memory or indiscriminate redundant filling, which are neutral or harmful. The ordering reproduces on retention seeds 6–9 and under 5% whole-packet backup loss. The result is a communication-execution-layer result achieved by ordinary rules, which any higher-layer planner (MPC or tool-using agent) inherits; no LLM is used.

**Index terms**—emergency/disaster networks, intermittent backhaul, LoRaWAN Class A, satellite short message, scheduling under limited service opportunities, age/timeliness, marginal-coverage/max-coverage packing.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Setting

We study a pre-event, always-on mountain geohazard monitoring deployment: field sensor nodes on a slope reach one cached, locally-autonomous gateway over LoRaWAN Class A; the gateway reaches the operations center over an intermittent cellular primary backhaul plus a constrained short-message backup. Nodes are solar-powered with small batteries and no mains. The service target is **continuity of monitoring obligations**, not an always-up link: a routine obligation for object *i* opens a window of length *P*, and is satisfied when one valid in-window sample is received at the center by the deadline window+grace; event obligations are short local-trigger bursts. The deployment class follows the Wang Guizhou loess-slope system [cite: Wang, Front. Earth Sci. 2022], the Kumar asymmetric multi-WAN deployment [cite: Springer MONET 2019], and alpine sites [cite: Hochvogel EGU25-11121]; the reference instance, obligations and evidence grades are fixed by the project task contract. Node count follows the v1.1 manifest (main table: 14 nodes = 1 gateway + 13 displacement stations, all SF7); 5/9-node runs are scale axes only.

### 1.2 Two decision levels, and where adaptation does and does not pay

A sample crosses two segments with very different availability: the **access** segment (node→gateway, intermittent LoRa, Class A, delayed downlink) and the **backhaul** segment (gateway→center, cellular primary + sparse short-message backup). This split makes two distinct control questions:
- **Upstream configuration.** Choose sampling interval and reporting period over time, under energy and the Class-A command delay. An extensive literature on AoI/energy-harvesting sampling motivates adaptive, freshness-driven loops [cite: Bacinoglu arXiv:1905.06679; Zakeri arXiv:2311.06522].
- **Backup content arbitration.** At each sparse backup opportunity, with a hard byte cap, choose *which already-collected samples* to put in the packet.

Our central empirical claim is that in this deployment the answers are opposite in character. Upstream, the optimum is pinned to a unique physical operating point by energy (sensing costs ≈20× a transmission) and by access/backup throughput, so dynamic reconfiguration has no measurable headroom (Finding I). Downstream, under hard backup scarcity, *which obligations' samples are sent* is decisive and item-level rules leave large, recoverable service on the table (Finding II). The two are not contradictory: they delimit the boundary between where a fixed execution layer suffices and where obligation-level content selection is required.

### 1.3 Contributions

All claims are inside the fixed scenario: no scenario substitution, no LLM in the loop, baselines instantiated at full strength and never weakened to win, criteria written before the confirming runs.

- **C1 — Grounded harness and a zero-copy failover layer.** A byte-/energy-accurate two-segment Class-A model with real obligation semantics and a primary/backup failover plane that is bit-identical to the v1.1 baseline when backup is disabled (five regression anchors).
- **C2 — Finding I: bounded headroom of upstream adaptation (a boundary result).** The strongest feasible static point reaches 0.984 and dominates adaptive rules; a rolling MPC that uses only nominal harvesting and declared link probabilities reaches 0.647; an obligation-driven joint policy reaches 0.937 with ≈80× commands and high seed variance, and its sampling and report-only ablations coincide, i.e., the sampling degree of freedom is empty. We state this as a scoped boundary, not "closed loops are bad," and withdrew earlier over-strong claims per independent review; the surviving statement is about *physical pinning* of the operating point, demonstrated with corrected arrival-time accounting.
- **C3 — Finding II (primary): obligation-level marginal-coverage packing.** We (i) describe scarcity on two axes (capacity and opportunity spacing), including a regime with capacity above production that still fails for lack of opportunities; (ii) show no single item-level rule dominates across regimes and trace each rule's failure; (iii) apply a textbook marginal-coverage/recency greedy packer, **maxcov**, which at identical byte cost beats the strongest item-level rule by +9.1/+11.7 pp in the two capacity-scarce regimes, with a no-redundant-filler variant (`cover2`) best in the sparse-large-packet regime, and reproduce on retention seeds and under 5% loss; and (iv) use repaired, witness-verified ablations to attribute the gain to obligation-level marginal coverage, in-packet marginal recomputation, and non-stalling, while showing cross-packet global memory and indiscriminate redundant filling are *not* required. A hand-built two-layer predecessor (`cover`) is retained as a replaceable implementation and is not the headline mechanism.
- **C4 — Scope of the agentic layer.** The communication execution layer needs no agent; a higher-layer agent/MPC inherits a correct substrate. This paper supplies the static-control/packet-selection evidence chapter for the single-paper route (doc33 candidate C, *execution-grounded mission compilation*); we position, but do not here assert, agent value at the mission-to-execution translation layer, and we do not re-run the previously falsified "LLM scores record importance" test.

### 1.4 Pre-registered kill criteria and a reported boundary

Finding II would be falsified by a legal-information (no future truth, identical backup budget) item-level rule that matches the marginal packer in every scarcity regime, by an ablation whose removal leaves actions unchanged (a dead component), or by the advantage disappearing on retention seeds. The R20 repair in fact triggered one such outcome for the *hand-built* rule: once the textbook maxcov-recency baseline was fielded, it matched or beat `cover`, so per the pre-registered decision we adopt the ordinary rule rather than rescue `cover`. We also report a boundary we did **not** engineer away: in *saturation* item-level newest-first/EDF reach 0.984 while marginal packing trails by ≈2.4 pp (maxcov 0.960), the cost of preferring coverage breadth when pure timeliness already saturates.

## 2. Related work

**AoI / energy-harvesting sampling.** Threshold/switching policies on age and battery assume a *sender-local* controller with immediate per-action feedback [cite: Bacinoglu arXiv:1905.06679; Arafa arXiv:2007.10200; TCOM/TII]. Joint sampling/transmission/energy under partial observability is cast as a POMDP with the controller at the transmitter, acting in the same slot [cite: Zakeri arXiv:2311.06522]. We field strong instances of these as baselines; Finding I delineates where their sender-local assumption is violated by the two-segment path, without claiming the policies are wrong in their native model.

**Scheduling with bounded buffers / limited service opportunities; max-coverage packing.** Constrained-queue models with exogenous arrivals, finite buffers, a server that visits objects at limited opportunities, and receipt feedback supply the formal elements we inherit (ICLDC, arXiv:2504.14556 / IEEE IoT-J, DOI 10.1109/JIOT.2025.3615410—verified as a single-hop UAV data-collection scheduler with an LLM in-context policy; it has no primary/backup dual-scale link, no many-to-many sample-obligation matching with absolute deadlines and grace, and no outage held at a gateway that sees only link receipts). Greedy coverage maximization under a knapsack/byte budget is a standard construction; our `maxcov` is its direct application—marginal newly-covered obligations per byte, recomputed after each pick, ties by deadline then recency, residual bytes filled newest-first. The contribution is not a new packing heuristic; it is showing *that this ordinary construction is the decisive control action in a specific intermittent-backhaul setting where item-level scheduling fails*, alongside the precise regime boundary.

**DTN / space links and constrained short-message packing.** Store-and-forward [cite: Fall SIGCOMM 2003] and schedule-aware bundle routing over *planned* contacts (CCSDS 734.3-B-1, SABR: route computation under stable topology and scheduled—not opportunistic—connectivity) address forwarding over sparse contacts; SABR chooses a contact/route, not which obligation's samples fill a packet. Applied BeiDou short-message designs already pack a capacity-limited message by a **single static priority**—fixed traffic classes (high-precision return > retransmission > routine), a per-sample anomaly/deviation score, or marking the first packets of a fragmented image as critical—and report a civil-IC user payload around 104 B in a 124 B frame [cite: BeiDou short-message patents; Wuhan Univ. J. Inf. Sci., LoRa+BDS]. We therefore explicitly do **not** claim content-selective packing as new; these are prior art for "fill the scarce packet by priority." Our object differs on combined axes they do not address: dual-scale primary/backup channels with a separate access segment; per-obligation absolute deadlines with grace and **many-to-many** candidate samples (one obligation has several time-competing candidates, one sample may match adjacent obligations); and a gateway that during an outage holds only link receipts, never the center's final satisfaction state. Under those axes a *static* per-sample priority is insufficient and the marginal-coverage objective with non-stalling flow is what recovers service. Contact-aware triage at extreme low rate (e.g., SHETLAND-NET) is the closest DTN instance but its primary record was not retrievable here and is cited only as a placeholder pending verification.

**Agents/LLMs for emergency networks.** The TopoLLM lineage [cite: DC Networks 12(2026) 273–282] and its three branches (LLM direct resource scheduling; LLM-assisted RL; post-disaster infrastructure recovery agents) let an agent choose network resources or recovery sequences *after* a disaster. Closely related mission-construction/repair agents (ICG-Restore; OSS-GPT; intent-based networking for 6G, arXiv:2601.06640) already provide intent objects, budget/time/precondition checks, and local repair over real network APIs; we therefore do not claim "natural language → constraints → tools" as a contribution, and we reserve the agentic claim for the execution-grounded mission-compilation layer of doc33 candidate C. The present paper is deliberately agent-free at the execution layer and makes no "first/uncovered" claim.

## 3. System model and problem

### 3.1 Deployment and roles
The reference instance has 14 nodes (1 gateway `gw0` + 13 displacement stations, all SF7); 5/9-node instances are scale axes. The gateway caches data and preloaded local-autonomy rules; nothing is mains-powered. Samples are displacement (6 B) or rainfall (4 B); aggregate steady obligation production is **84 obligations/h at P=600 s** (13 displacement stations × 6/h plus the gateway rainfall obligations), the figure used for capacity normalization.

### 3.2 Two-segment communication and Class-A delay
A configuration command (`set_sampling_interval`, `set_report_period`) can only be emitted in a Class-A receive window opened by an uplink and takes effect after the next sampling cycle (dwell 300 s, command TTL 6 h); during a backhaul outage the center's downlink gate is closed, so foreknowledge of an outage cannot be acted on from the center—feed-forward must execute at the gateway over local LoRa. Access arrival probability is 0.74 and the primary backhaul is good with probability 0.62 in the reference instance.

### 3.3 Energy
Per-tick SoC = harvest − sleep/sense/TX/RX; sensing is 4.7e-4 Wh/sample (measured profile [cite: Ragnoli JLPEA 2022]) and an uplink 2.33e-5 Wh (**sensing ≈20× a report**); capacity 0.05 Wh. A node whose SoC falls below one sensing energy is declared dead for the mission (the restart/energy model is fixed by R01/R02 and loss is split into never-at-gateway / late-at-gateway / missed-to-center classes).

### 3.4 Obligations and service metric
Routine obligation *i* has window [iP,(i+1)P], grace P, deadline (i+2)P; a sample matches deterministically from its `taken_at` (the same `matches` function is used by the gateway policy and the center scorer—there is no label noise in matching). The shared exogenous denominator is identical across policies. Primary metric: **timely routine coverage** = obligations with an in-window sample received by the deadline; we also report collection rate, nodes alive, final SoC, uplink airtime, downlink commands, and backup packets/bytes (real cost).

### 3.5 Observable plane
A legal controller knows link state/history, its own queues, sample heard/taken times, and public obligation cadence (period/grace) and the configured backup opportunity grid; it never knows future outage truth or the center's final per-obligation satisfaction during an outage. The backup leg returns only a link-level send receipt; in our model a sent backup is received (success=1, with a separate 0.95 study), so "I sent it" and "the center has it" coincide at the link layer—but the gateway still cannot know *which obligation the center will treat a many-to-many candidate as finally satisfying relative to other samples*.

### 3.6 Problem
Per obligation, over time, choose (a) sampling/reporting configuration under energy and Class-A delay, and (b) which pending samples occupy each capacity-capped backup opportunity, using legal history only, to maximize timely coverage under energy and backup-byte budgets. We compare under identical exogenous trajectories and budgets.

## 4. Execution layer and policy families

### 4.1 Zero-copy primary/backup failover
The plane subclasses the v1.1 control plane and overrides only `backhaul_forward`. When the primary is down, at each backup opportunity (t mod r_b = 0) it packetizes one subset of pending samples at sample granularity (net payload = backup bytes − 20 B header), splits a source item across packets, and removes sent samples from the primary queue to avoid duplication; a `last_primary_ok_at` updates only on genuine primary forwards. With backup disabled it is bit-identical to v1.1 (anchor). Backup rate/payload ({120,300,600,900,1200} s; {78,200} B) are scanned as A-level research assumptions; a civil BeiDou IC frame reports 124 B total / ≈104 B user payload [cite: Wuhan Univ. J.], which brackets our payloads, but the ≥1 min cadence and ≤200 B figures come from different standard sections and are not fused into one "standard operating point."

### 4.2 Upstream policy family (Finding I)
Open-loop fixed grids `grid(S,R)`; v1.1 adaptive report policies; an Energy-AoI loop `ea_aoi`; an ordinary **rolling MPC** that searches the legal sampling/reporting grid each cycle using only nominal harvesting and declared link probabilities (it does not read the future); and an obligation-driven policy **ODP** with sampling-gating and report-only ablations. Every fed signal passes an identifiability gate (a signal whose constant setting leaves actions unchanged is declared dead, not retained).

### 4.3 Backup chooser family and the marginal packer (Finding II)
Item-level baselines: **fifo** (heard order), **edf** (earliest obligation deadline), **latest** (newest heard). Obligation-level item rules: **obligation** (cross-packet de-duplication + EDF; once an obligation has been sent a qualifying sample, later copies are suppressed), **salvage** (also hard-drop samples past their local deadline).

The principal packer is the textbook greedy **maxcov-recency** (`maxcov`). Each opportunity it maintains the set of obligations already covered using only the same per-send/per-receipt history visible to every chooser, then:
```
phase 1 (marginal coverage), repeated within the packet:
  among samples that still fit, compute marginal = number of not-yet-covered
  obligations (tightest deadline > now) that this sample newly satisfies
  take the sample maximizing marginal / byte; ties: earliest deadline, then newest heard
  recompute marginal after every pick
phase 2 (recency fill):
  fill any residual bytes with the remaining samples newest-heard first
never return an empty packet while pending samples exist (no stall)
```
A two-layer hand-built predecessor, **cover**, and its variants are kept for attribution and are all genuinely dispatched after the R20 repair (with branch/behavior witnesses): `cover` (a strict layer-0 disjoint-breadth pass then newest-first fill), `cover_l0only` (layer 0 only, no fill), `cover_local` (coverage set reset every packet, i.e., no cross-packet memory), and `cover2` (the fill phase admits only samples that still carry a not-yet-covered obligation—no purely-redundant filler). `maxcov` and `cover2` are the same marginal-coverage family; they differ only in whether residual bytes are spent on redundant copies, which separates capacity-scarce from opportunity-sparse regimes (§6).

## 5. Finding I — upstream dynamic configuration is physically pinned

*Regime: P=600 s, 16 h backhaul outage, solar peak 0.03 Wh/h, initial SoC 1, 14 nodes, backup 300 s/78 B; 6 paired seeds (R05, `v3joint_r05_headroom.json`); held-out seeds 6–9 (R07).*

**The feasible static point dominates.** `grid600x600` reaches **0.9841** [0.981,0.987] (collection 1.000, final SoC 0.265, 26 commands, 691 s airtime). Slower sampling loses obligations outright (`grid1800x900` 0.597 with collection only 0.694); slower reporting `grid900x600` reaches 0.953; a fixed 900 s report 0.868; the Energy-AoI loop 0.873. The ordinary rolling MPC reaches only **0.647** (collection 0.841): it treats the link at a constant nominal probability, does not use the deterministic outage schedule, and under weak energy backs off into missed collection. The obligation-driven ODP reaches 0.937 [0.820,0.989] but with ≈2,093 commands (≈80×) and 951 s airtime, and one seed at 0.820 from over-reacting to unreliable replica feedback; crucially **ODP_joint (0.937) ≈ ODP_report_only (0.936)**—the sampling degree of freedom is empty and "joint acquisition×backhaul" reduces to report timing.

**Mechanism.** Sensing is the dominant energy cost and cannot be denser than the obligation period without killing nodes under weak harvesting; reporting is matched to access/backup throughput; deviating inside the outage window (faster 300 s report self-congests against backup throughput; slower report starves the backup of records; relaxed 1800 s sampling cuts backup feed) is dominated at every deviation we scanned. A center-issued feed-forward segment plan is bit-identical to static because the center's downlink gate is closed during the outage; only gateway-local execution is actionable, and there the optimum is the single matched point (P,P). Held-out seeds 6–9 preserve the ordering: grid 0.9830, ODP 0.9010 [0.743,0.988], rolling MPC 0.6193, in-900 0.9430.

**The backup leg is what keeps this regime at 0.98 rather than 0.42.** With backup on, coverage is 0.9841; turning backup off drops it to **0.4188** and *raises* uplink airtime from 691 s to 3,101 s (LoRa self-retransmission against a closed backhaul); the backup leg carries 2,467 records and closes 1,248 displacement obligations inside the 16 h window (R06). This motivates Finding II: once the upstream point is fixed, the remaining service is decided by what the sparse backup carries.

## 6. Finding II — obligation-level marginal packing over the sparse backup

### 6.1 Two scarcity axes (R08)
Steady production is 84 obligations/h. Net backup records/h = (3600/rate)·⌊(bytes−20)/6⌋. Along the capacity axis, item-level packing sits on a 0.45–0.53 plateau when net throughput is below production and saturates near 0.98 once it exceeds production; but capacity alone is not sufficient—**r1200/200 provides 90 net records/h (≈1.15× the 78-record/h displacement production used in the early scan) yet reaches only 0.528**, because it offers one ≈30-record packet every 1,200 s and obligations whose deadline falls between opportunities cannot be helped by aggregate throughput. We therefore describe regimes on two axes, **capacity** and **opportunity spacing**, and do not claim a single-axis transition at load 1. The four main regimes are: saturated r300/78; capacity-scarce r600/78 (0.69×) and r900/78 (0.46×); and opportunity-sparse r1200/200. **Fig. 1(a)** shows both axes, with r1200/200 annotated as capacity-sufficient yet opportunity-sparse.

### 6.2 No single item-level rule wins, and why each fails
Under scarcity (6 development seeds, `v3joint_r20_ablation_fix.json`):

| regime | edf | obligation | latest | salvage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| r600/78B (0.69× cap) | 0.504 | **0.838** | 0.548 | 0.769 |
| r900/78B (0.46× cap) | 0.462 | 0.574 | 0.477 | **0.656** |
| r1200/200B (sparse opp.) | 0.528 | 0.804 | **0.917** | 0.711 |

Item-level rules waste scarce packets on repeated copies of the same obligations or on the wrong objective. `obligation` de-duplicates efficiently in the small-packet regime but is capped at **0.838 even when backup is saturated** (r300): once it marks an obligation "sent" it suppresses later copies and, when the queue looks fully covered, **skips the send opportunity entirely**, emitting far fewer packets; per-obligation pairing against `latest` shows the *same samples eventually arrive* but arrive late—maximum gateway wait 41,400 s versus ≤300 s for non-stalling rules. `salvage`'s hard expiry helps only at the tightest extreme and otherwise drops samples the center still counts; the "unreliable link needs retransmission" explanation is ruled out (backup success=1), as is any matching-error explanation (matching is deterministic and shared). The recurring failure is a **coverage/timeliness** failure under many-to-many candidates, compounded by stall-on-covered.

### 6.3 Marginal packing: main paired result (R20; Fig. 1(b))
Same six development seeds, identical backup budget:

| regime | edf | obligation | latest | salvage | cover | cover2 | **maxcov** | vs best item rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| saturated r300/78 | 0.984 | 0.838 | **0.984** | 0.771 | 0.978 | 0.978 | 0.960 | −2.4 pp (reported boundary) |
| r600/78 | 0.504 | 0.838 | 0.548 | 0.769 | 0.909 | 0.919 | **0.929** [0.908,0.937] | **+9.1 pp** |
| r900/78 | 0.462 | 0.574 | 0.477 | 0.656 | 0.698 | 0.699 | **0.773** [0.726,0.800] | **+11.7 pp** |
| r1200/200 | 0.528 | 0.804 | 0.917 | 0.711 | 0.922 | **0.953** [0.944,0.960] | 0.922 | **+3.6 pp** (cover2 vs latest) |

The marginal-coverage family is the strongest group in every non-saturated regime, with no single item-level rule competitive everywhere. `maxcov` wins both capacity-scarce regimes; `cover2`—which refuses to spend sparse large-packet bytes on purely redundant copies—wins the opportunity-sparse regime. In saturation item-level EDF/newest-first already saturate and marginal packing trails by ≈2.4 pp, a genuine regime boundary. **Fig. 1** (`results/fig_backup_arbitration.png`): (a) the two scarcity axes; (b) per-regime coverage of all choosers at identical budget, with maxcov outlined and cover2 hatched.

### 6.4 What is actually necessary (repaired ablations, R20)
Earlier ablation runs were invalid—the variant names dispatched to the EDF fallback (and `cover_local` had never been implemented). After repairing the dispatcher into one unified engine, adding synthetic single-opportunity and end-to-end branch/behavior witnesses, and joining per-obligation outcomes by the stable `oid` (4,053 obligations, unique keys, identical row counts), the r600/seed-0 paired differences are:
- **Obligation-level marginal coverage is the main gain.** maxcov nets +1,523 obligations over edf and +1,380 over newest-first (0.937 vs 0.565/0.605 timely coverage).
- **In-packet recomputation plus the recency fill (non-stalling) are necessary.** The full packer nets +528 obligations over a layer-0-only pass that does not fill residual bytes (0.92 vs 0.79).
- **Cross-packet global memory is NOT necessary.** Resetting the coverage set every packet (`cover_local`) is statistically tied with global memory (120 vs 132 paired differences) and is markedly better in the tightest regime (r900: 0.769 vs 0.698), where global memory prematurely removes fresh updates from the marginal phase.
- **Indiscriminate redundant filling is NOT necessary.** Refusing purely-redundant filler (`cover2`) is tied or better everywhere and decisively better for sparse large packets (r1200: 0.953 vs 0.922). The previously claimed "redundancy insurance" mechanism is withdrawn.
`maxcov` and `cover2` are tied with each other (94 vs 89 paired differences); their only difference is whether phase 2 spends bytes on redundancy, which is exactly the capacity-vs-opportunity distinction. No retained component is a dead parameter.

### 6.5 Same cost and retention-seed reproduction
The marginal packer does not buy service with budget (r600 maxcov 13,557 B vs edf 13,564 B, 174 packets; r1200 cover2 14,196 B vs latest 16,710 B—fewer bytes, by not sending redundancy). On **retention seeds 6–9** (these seeds were previously used in R07, so they are "retained during chooser selection," not a never-seen test set) the ordering is unchanged (`v3joint_r20_heldout69.json`): r600 maxcov 0.918 vs strongest item 0.801 (+11.7 pp), r900 maxcov 0.747 vs 0.639 (+10.7 pp), r1200 cover2 0.937 vs latest 0.923; saturation newest-first 0.983.

### 6.6 Distance to a relaxed-resource reference
Relaxing only the byte cap (unlimited payload, real sparse opportunity grid) gives a *relaxed-resource policy reference* of 0.966–0.984—we do not call it a proven optimal upper bound: even with unlimited bandwidth, discrete opportunities plus stochastic LoRa arrival leave 2–3% unrecoverable. At the extreme r900 (capacity 0.46 of production) even the best packer reaches only 0.75–0.77: packing cannot create physical capacity, and the residual gap is a capacity hard limit rather than a packing error. A tighter *closed-loop* offline optimum cannot be obtained by fixing one arrival stream and re-packing it, because a chooser feeds back through the primary queue/Class-A windows and changes a fraction of first-heard times; we therefore make no optimality claim and leave a fully coupled closed-loop optimum as future work.

### 6.7 Robustness to an unreliable backup (R20)
Setting whole-packet success to 0.95 (the standard's ≥95% floor; i.i.d., no implicit retransmission; a default-1.0 switch leaves every regression anchor bit-identical) preserves the ordering (`v3joint_r20_loss95_maxcov.json`):

We claim robustness only to i.i.d. 5% whole-packet loss, not to correlated or deeper fades.

## 7. Discussion

**A boundary, not a panacea.** Finding I says dynamic *upstream* configuration has no headroom once energy and throughput pin the operating point; Finding II says *downstream content* arbitration has substantial headroom precisely under backup scarcity, and that the winning tool is an ordinary marginal-coverage packer rather than a bespoke heuristic. The identifiability question—does this control action change a deliverable outcome, using only legal information?—locates an operator on either side. We explicitly do not claim results for value-heterogeneous obligations (we use fixed, value-equal routine duties), monetary quota constraints, multiple backup channels, or operation beyond single-gateway access capacity; those are registered, not silently folded in.

**Why an ordinary rule winning is the result.** A communication paper does not need an irreplaceable LLM or a novel scheduler. A failover substrate that cannot be polluted by a downstream outage, plus the observation that—under two-segment many-to-many obligations with link-receipt-only feedback and hard packet scarcity—a textbook marginal-coverage/recency packer recovers 9–12 pp of service at the same byte cost while item rules stall, is a communication-systems characterization that any higher-layer planner inherits. That a hand-built two-layer predecessor was replaced by the textbook rule after a fair comparison is a feature of the method, not a loss.

**Where the agent goes (C4).** The execution layer needs no agent. The agentic claim of the single-paper route is one level up (doc33 candidate C): compiling changing, externally authorized monitoring requirements into plans that are *achievable* across acquisition/opportunity/energy constraints and emitting structured infeasibility/repair when they are not. Closest prior agents already implement intent objects, precondition checks and local repair (ICG-Restore; OSS-GPT; IBN6G), so the claim must rest on communication-specific execution grounding, not on "natural language → tools"; the static-control and packet-selection results above are the substrate and the strongest ordinary baselines against which that layer is judged.

**Threats to validity.** (i) Backup cadence/payload are A-level assumptions from different standard sections (DZ/T 0450 §7.4.2.3/§7.4.3.2), scanned not asserted; RDSS cost/power lack a verbatim source, and text was obtained via a document-sharing mirror (official site TLS failed locally). (ii) The main table uses backup success=1; a separate i.i.d. 0.95 study preserves ordering, but correlated/deeper fades and an explicit ACK-retransmission mechanism are not modeled; the model's synchronous node `ack()` on center receipt is a stated simplification, not evidence that "center received but node did not know" is modeled. (iii) Harvesting is a model swept over peak/initial SoC, not a site trace. (iv) Quantitative claims rest on 6 development + 4 retention paired seeds; phase scans are exploratory for trend, and seeds 6–9 are not a never-seen test set. (v) We never label rain-driven acceleration a false precursor; geotechnical truth is left to node autonomy. (vi) The relaxed-resource reference is not a proven optimum, and a tightly coupled closed-loop optimum is left open. No "first/gap" claim is made.

## 8. Conclusion

In a pre-disaster monitoring network with a Class-A access segment, intermittent cellular backhaul, and a narrow short-message backup, the two levels of network control behave oppositely. *Upstream* sampling/reporting adaptation is physically pinned to the obligation-period operating point and is dominated by a feasible static configuration plus failover (0.984); the apparent "joint" freedom collapses to report timing. *Downstream*, once the backup is the bottleneck, item-level scheduling leaves large service on the table, and an ordinary obligation-level marginal-coverage/recency packer—recomputing marginal coverage within each packet, filling residual bytes newest-first, and never stalling—recovers 9–12 pp of timely coverage at the same byte cost across capacity-scarce regimes, with a no-redundant-filler variant best under opportunity sparsity, reproducing on retention seeds and under 5% loss; in saturation item-level rules already suffice. The combined statement maps where a fixed communication execution layer suffices and where obligation-level content selection is required—a stable substrate on which the mission-compilation layer of an agent runtime can be built and evaluated, rather than an unstable one it must constantly repair.
