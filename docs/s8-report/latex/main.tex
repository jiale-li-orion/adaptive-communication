\begin{abstract}
When a mountain-site backhaul fails, the network does not freeze: communication state that was
installed \emph{before} the failure keeps executing. Unacknowledged sensor records continue to be
retransmitted and fill the few recovery access batches, and a dense sampling configuration that can
no longer be countermanded keeps draining a battery through the night. We study where, and on what
evidence, these two forms of \emph{persistent installed state} can be terminated after the control
path dies, for a battery-plus-solar LoRaWAN Class~A deployment with an autonomous gateway, a
cellular primary and a rate-limited BeiDou short-message backup. We formulate one decision
problem---local termination under partial evidence---in which a node, holding only its clock,
battery, cache, recent access receipts, and bounds supplied at install time, must either retain or
release a record and either hold or relax a configuration, subject to no-false-release, energy
survival, locality, and no-false-completion constraints. For records the termination time is the
obligation's business deadline, which the node knows unconditionally; we show the resulting
source-local rule is bit-identical to standard Bundle-Protocol per-record expiry (BPv7 lifetime),
yet its \emph{cross-segment placement} decides the gain: source-local expiry raises outage on-time
delivery 202$\to$434 and 2.52-fold over ten seeds, while suppressing expired sends at the gateway
alone back-pressures access ($-8$). For configurations the termination time is \emph{not} locally
known---it depends on mission revisions that travel on the failed path and on return-slot geometry
held at the gateway. A fixed clock night-guard suffices when the outage aligns with sunset and
energy is ample, but under overcast skies it leaves 20--31 nodes to discharge fatally; a fixed
config TTL that is short enough to save those nodes falsely releases 183 still-deliverable
obligations in a different outage phase. A \emph{delivery-bounded lease}, whose installed bound is
the last time the current profile's data can still meet an obligation (deadline aligned to a return
slot), removes the deaths in every phase without false release. These mechanisms are ordinary
primitives with a re-derived bound; an LLM controller is used only formatively and reveals the
interface fault---LoRa access receipts read as backhaul state---that the design targets. All
claims are from deterministic, repository-reproducible runs; end-to-end and cross-model agent
validation is specified as future work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{A concrete failure, not an abstraction.}
Consider a field node running an authorised dense sampling profile. While the cellular backhaul is
up the centre sends the configuration and the node applies it at its next LoRaWAN Class~A receive
window. The backhaul then fails for sixteen hours. Three things keep happening with no control path
to stop them. First, the node keeps sampling and transmitting at the dense rate, drawing energy the
battery may not have through the night; a downgrade authored after the failure cannot reach it.
Second, records already cached at the node are, under the standard hold-until-acknowledged edge
discipline, retransmitted oldest-first on every narrow access opportunity, including records whose
business deadline has already passed. Third, the centre and gateway cannot tell whether the missing
data were never sampled, are sitting at a node, have reached the gateway, or simply lack a return
slot, and they cannot tell a command that is queued, in transit, or applied. The network is not
stuck; \emph{installed state is still executing}, and its execution spends energy, access slots,
and metered short-message bytes on work that can no longer meet any obligation.

This is a communication-systems failure with a specific shape: \textbf{the termination of a
persistent installed state depends on a control/acknowledgement path that has itself failed}.
SateRIoT observes that lossy satellite windows waste COTS-radio retransmissions and redesigns link
estimation and multi-hop sharing around that measured behaviour~\cite{sateriot}; semantic HARQ
reuses the mature HARQ primitive but changes its error criterion to match a new communication
object~\cite{razavivideo}. In the same spirit we do not seek a new primitive for its own sake. We
ask which ordinary mechanism already terminates each kind of installed state, and where a genuine
gap remains after the ordinary mechanisms are applied fairly.

\paragraph{Two kinds of persistent installed state.}
A \textbf{record state} is an unacknowledged sample in the node cache, occupying a slot in every
future access batch until it is acknowledged or discarded. A \textbf{configuration state} is the
node's current sampling/reporting profile, installed by an applied command, which continuously
drives energy and airtime until a newer command or a local condition ends it. Both are created while
connected and both outlive the connection. They differ in what evidence terminates them: a record's
useful lifetime ends at an absolute business deadline the source can compute from the public mission
cadence, whereas a configuration's useful lifetime ends when the data it produces can no longer be
delivered---a fact that depends on mission \emph{revisions} (which reach the field over the failed
backhaul) and on the geometry of future return opportunities (which the gateway, not the node,
holds). This asymmetry, made precise in \S\ref{sec:problem}, is the paper's technical centre.

\paragraph{What strong ordinary mechanisms already settle.}
Before proposing anything we assemble deliberately strong baselines with the same legal
information: energy-aware feed-forward sampling, an omniscient offline packer, rolling-horizon
MPC, max-coverage/recency backup packing, earliest-deadline-first and obligation-greedy queues,
piecewise non-prescient mission views, and symmetric resource relaxations. Over the policy families
we tested, re-optimising collection, scheduling, or packing under fixed resources yields no robust
on-time-delivery gain; the large fixed-resource gains require adding backhaul capacity or energy.
We report this as a scoped negative result, not as an optimality theorem. Two ordinary termination
mechanisms also work well. \emph{(i)~Per-record expiry.} Releasing a cached record when its business
deadline passes is standard Bundle-Protocol lifetime management~\cite{rfc9171}; we prove a prior
``certificate-driven purge'' is bit-identical to it. \emph{(ii)~A local clock/energy guard.} A
node-local rule that relaxes dense sampling at dusk removes overnight deaths whenever the outage
aligns with sunset and the battery is adequate. We explicitly credit these as mature primitives.

\paragraph{The residual that is not settled.}
Two controlled experiments isolate what the ordinary mechanisms do \emph{not} cover.
(1)~Releasing expired records at the \emph{gateway} (stop sending them, leave the source cache
untouched) withholds the acknowledgement, so the expired record keeps filling the node's access
batch; on-time delivery moves \emph{down} 202$\to$194. The disposition must be consistent across
the access and backhaul segments, which single-hop AoI discard and hop-local bundle expiry do not
address. (2)~The clock guard acts only at dusk. When an authorised daytime downgrade falls inside an
outage, the node runs the dense profile for hours after its data have ceased to be deliverable;
under overcast skies this lag is fatal (20--31 nodes), yet a naive short config TTL that relaxes
early enough to save those nodes falsely releases obligations still deliverable over the backup in
a differently phased outage (183 obligations lost). No fixed TTL value is safe and sufficient across
phases. The residual is therefore not ``terminate state''---ordinary expiry and guards do
that---but \textbf{computing the configuration's termination bound from delivery geometry the node
does not have, and installing it while connected so it can be enforced locally while disconnected}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{A single termination problem for persistent installed state
(\S\ref{sec:model},\S\ref{sec:problem}).} We model records and configurations as state installed
while connected and executed while disconnected, define each state's release time from the
obligation chain, and state one local decision problem under four constraints (no-false-release,
energy survival, evidence locality, no-false-completion), with a three-class
confirmed/unconfirmed/unreachable statement discipline that makes \emph{unknown} a first-class
outcome.
\item \textbf{Record termination is standard expiry; cross-segment placement is the gain
(\S\ref{sec:mech-record},\S\ref{sec:eval}).} We prove deadline-aligned source expiry is
bit-identical to BPv7 per-record lifetime on ten seeds and isolate the system contribution---an
end-to-end business-deadline basis and source/gateway consistency---which doubles outage on-time
delivery and drives expired backup records to 1, while gateway-only suppression moves cost to the
access segment. A full-horizon time-aware attribution corrects an earlier accounting that had
labelled 830 late-access failures as backhaul-capacity failures.
\item \textbf{A delivery-bounded configuration lease for the residual
(\S\ref{sec:mech-lease},\S\ref{sec:eval}).} We reuse the ordinary config-TTL/lease primitive but
replace its fixed-duration bound with the last deliverable time of the current profile's data
(obligation deadline aligned to a primary/backup return slot), computed at the gateway while
connected and enforced at the node with no downlink. Across outage phases and harvest levels it
removes the 20--31 overcast-sky deaths the clock guard leaves and avoids the 183-obligation false
release of the best fixed TTL, while in ample-energy regimes it only removes wasted dense work.
\item \textbf{A formative real-agent study that locates the interface fault
(\S\ref{sec:eval-agent}).} Symmetric accounting of 1089 live LLM decisions shows LoRa access
receipts being read as backhaul state in both optimistic and pessimistic directions; an
unknown-aware projector removes the healthy-link false assertions in offline replay. The agent is a
\emph{controller candidate}, not part of the problem definition; end-to-end and cross-model runs are
specified as future work.
\end{enumerate}
We do not claim an LLM out-optimises a solver, a new discard algorithm, or a global scheduling
upper bound. The deterministic mechanisms are in-layer tools available equally to rules, solvers,
and agents.

\section{Background, scenario, and standards grounding}
\label{sec:bg}

\paragraph{Deployment.}
Fourteen sensor nodes in two groups monitor displacement (and rainfall) on a slope. Nodes sample
and upload to a local gateway over LoRaWAN Class~A; the gateway forwards to a central server over
cellular backhaul, with a BeiDou short-message service as a sparse, metered backup that carries
field-to-centre messages only (it is not a downlink configuration channel). Nodes are battery plus
small solar. The mission is pre-disaster and routine, with authorised warning-level changes; no
component here judges landslide risk. Node count, role mapping, and disturbance parameters follow
the frozen Task v1.1 instance manifest; an earlier 16-node sketch is not used as a field fact.

\paragraph{Standards-grounded authority.}
We use two Chinese geological-hazard monitoring industry standards, DZ/T~0460--2023 and
DZ/T~0450--2023, retrieved and clause-audited in the repository~\cite{dzt0460,dzt0450}. Relevant
clauses: \S5.3.3 permits remote dynamic adjustment of sampling and upload frequency by warning
level (no numeric levels are given, so the periods we use are declared research choices);
\S8.4.1 defines four levels (red/orange/yellow/blue) and \S8.4.2 permits upgrade, downgrade, or
termination after a conference; \S5.3.7 calls for dual-mode communication under weak coverage in
high-risk extreme weather---a conditional clause that appears once, not a blanket dual-channel
mandate, and the communication-layer standard does not impose it. The BeiDou open-service
specification (BDS-OS-PS-3.0)~\cite{bdsps3} reports $\geq$95\% success, average latency under 2~s,
an average service frequency of one message per 30~s, and a single-message ceiling of 14{,}000
bits, while noting that realised frequency and maximum length are constrained by registration
parameters---the rate is a provisioned quantity. These clauses bound what the system may lawfully
do: re-compile frequency within granted levels and request a conference change, never silently
drop an obligation or declare risk.

\paragraph{Operating point.}
The backup uses one 78-byte packet every 1200~s (20~B header, 58~B payload; a displacement sample is
6~B and rainfall 4~B, so roughly 9--10 samples fit per packet). The elevated (yellow) task uses a
300~s period with a delivery deadline two periods after window open, i.e.\ a $\approx$600~s delivery
window---shorter than the 1200~s backup slot spacing. The 200~B single-packet ceiling and the
$\geq$1~min period in DZ/T~0450 (\S7.4.3.2, \S7.4.2.3, with a $\geq$5~s inter-message gap at
\S7.4.3.5) belong to different service-formulation sections and are not combined into a single
``BeiDou operating point''; the 1200~s/78~B point is a declared research choice.

\section{System model}
\label{sec:model}

Time is slotted at $\Delta{=}60$~s; $t{=}0$ is 06:00 local, so $t{=}12$~h is 18:00 (sunset) and
the solar cycle is explicit. Sets of nodes $\mathcal N$, measurands $\mathcal M$, and a gateway $g$
compose the field; a centre $c$ lies beyond the backhaul.

\paragraph{Obligations and the delivery chain.}
A monitoring obligation is $o=(n,m,[l_o,h_o),d_o)$ over node $n$, measurand $m$, sampling window
and absolute delivery deadline. A routine obligation at period $P$ has $h_o-l_o=P$ and
$d_o=h_o+P$ (one period of grace). A sample $s$ fulfils $o$ only if it matches $(n,m)$, is taken in
window, and is received centrally by $d_o$. Each sample traverses
\[
\underbrace{\text{qualified sample}}_{\text{node}}
 \!\to\! \underbrace{\text{heard at }g}_{\text{LoRa access}}
 \!\to\! \underbrace{\text{returned by }d_o}_{\text{cellular / short-message}}
 \!\to\! \underbrace{\text{central evidence}}_{\text{fulfilled}} .
\]
For piecewise missions the first segment uses a closed interval and later segments half-open
windows, so a boundary sample cannot double-cover adjacent windows after a level change.

\paragraph{Links, outages, and who can act.}
The LoRa access link (node$\to$gateway, with Class~A downlink windows) succeeds per opportunity
with probability $p_a{=}0.74$; the primary backhaul (gateway$\to$centre) is good with probability
$p_b{=}0.62$ when not in a declared outage. A control outage is an interval
$\mathcal O=[a,b)$ during which the primary is unavailable to centre$\to$field traffic: centre
commands and mission-table segments are refused at admission (never transmitted), and the
short-message backup cannot carry downlink configuration. During $\mathcal O$ the only entities
that can change field communication behaviour are the \emph{node} (on its clock, own battery,
cache, recent access receipts, and bounds carried by already-applied commands) and the
\emph{gateway} (on samples it has actually heard, its forwarding log, and backup slots used). The
centre retains authority to authorise changes but no channel to install them; a revision authored
in $\mathcal O$ reaches the field only at $b$.

\paragraph{Persistent installed state.}
Two state types are installed while connected and executed while disconnected.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,nosep]
\item \emph{Record state.} Each unacknowledged sample $s$ at node $n$ has creation time
$t_s$, a business deadline $d(s)=(\lfloor t_s/P\rfloor+2)P$ under the locally known cadence, and
occupies one of $K{=}240$ cache slots and up to $B{=}32$ positions in an access batch. Under the
modelled edge discipline it leaves the cache only on central acknowledgement (modelled
synchronously without airtime, equally favouring every queue rule); until then it is re-sent
oldest-first.
\item \emph{Configuration state.} The node runs a profile $q(t)\in\{P_0,P_1\}$ (sparse
$P_0{=}600$~s, dense $P_1{=}300$~s) for both sampling and reporting, installed by an applied
command that carries a version. A command is \emph{applied} only when a later node report confirms
the new profile (Class~A latency); a queued or in-transit command changes nothing. While applied,
$q{=}P_1$ draws additional sampling and uplink energy every tick until something terminates it.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Energy.}
A sample costs $e_s{=}4.7{\times}10^{-4}$~Wh and an uplink $e_u{=}2.33{\times}10^{-5}$~Wh; battery
capacity is $C{=}0.05$~Wh. Solar input is a 12~h half-sine with peak $\eta$ (nominally
$0.03$~Wh/h) plus per-node/hour stochastic cloud occlusion. A node whose state of charge reaches
zero \emph{permanently} dies in the main configuration (an absorbing state). Holding dense
sampling over a 12~h night draws $\approx0.071$~Wh $>C$.

\paragraph{Release time of installed state.}
For each state $x$ define its release time $\trel(x)$ as the earliest time after which keeping $x$
alive can no longer contribute to a fulfilled obligation.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,nosep]
\item For a record, $\trel(s)=d(s)$: past the business deadline the record cannot fulfil any
obligation, so further retention or retransmission has zero delivery value.
\item For a configuration profile, $\trel(q)$ is the last deadline of an obligation the profile's
data can still meet through \emph{some} feasible path:
\begin{equation}
\trel(q)=\max_{o:\,\text{profile }q\text{ produces }o}\;
   \min\{\,d(o),\ \tau_{\text{last slot}}(o)\,\},
\label{eq:trelcfg}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_{\text{last slot}}(o)$ is the latest primary or backup return opportunity at which a
qualified sample for $o$ can be heard and forwarded by $d(o)$. Unlike $d(s)$, this bound combines
mission timing (which obligations the current profile serves) with return-slot geometry (when they
can be carried), neither of which is fully observable at the node during $\mathcal O$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Service loss.}
The objective counts deadline-valid obligation delivery (service), permanent node deaths, and the
resource cost of state that outlives its release time---wasted samples, access attempts, short-
message bytes, and energy. Offline, each unfulfilled obligation is charged to its \emph{first}
failing stage under an optimistic model that grants every later assumption, giving four mutually
exclusive segments: \senergy (no qualified sample), \saccess (sampled but not heard at the gateway
by the deadline), \stime (heard but no return opportunity exists), \scap (an opportunity exists but
capacity/congestion cannot carry it). An online observer that cannot see a stage must report
\emph{unknown}, never guess it.

\paragraph{Three statement classes.}
Every feasibility claim is one of: \textbf{confirmed} (an independent report shows the target
config, or a matching sample is received centrally); \textbf{unconfirmed} (a command is in flight
or receipts are incomplete---this implies neither that it took effect nor that the channel is
down); \textbf{unreachable} (the current path is known down from explicit network-side state). A
LoRa access receipt (``a node was heard'') is evidence about the access link only and can never by
itself establish backhaul reachability.

\section{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

During an outage $\mathcal O$, at each tick $t$ each acting entity chooses, for the state it can
touch, an action: for a record $a_t(s)\in\{\textsc{retain},\textsc{release}\}$; for the
configuration $a_t(q)\in\{\textsc{hold},\textsc{revert}\}$ (revert dense to the last confirmed
sparse profile). Let $\mathcal E_e(t)$ be the evidence entity $e$ lawfully holds at $t$ (for a node:
clock, own SoC, cache contents, recent Class~A receipts, and any bound carried by commands applied
before $a$; explicitly \emph{not} future mission-table segments, simulator-wide samples, or
backhaul truth). The local termination problem is
\begin{align}
\textbf{Problem 1.}\quad
\min_{\{a_t\}}\quad
 & \sum_o \mathbf 1[o\ \text{unfulfilled}]
   +\lambda_d\sum_n \mathbf 1[n\ \text{dies}]
   +\lambda_w\!\!\sum_{x:\,t\geq\trel(x)}\!\! \operatorname{cost}(x\ \text{kept}) \label{eq:obj}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
 & \text{(no-false-release)}\quad a_t(x)=\textsc{release/revert}\ \Rightarrow\
    t\geq\trel(x), \label{eq:c1}\\
 & \text{(energy-survival)}\quad \operatorname{SoC}_n(t)>0\ \ \forall n,t, \label{eq:c2}\\
 & \text{(locality)}\quad a_t\ \text{is a function only of}\ \mathcal E_e(t), \label{eq:c3}\\
 & \text{(no-false-completion)}\quad
    \parbox[t]{8.6cm}{\raggedright unknown feasibility $\Rightarrow$ report unconfirmed or unfulfillable, never fulfilled or unreachable.}\label{eq:c4}
\end{align}
The tension is between \eqref{eq:c1} and \eqref{eq:c2} under the locality restriction
\eqref{eq:c3}. Releasing too early violates \eqref{eq:c1} (work that could still be delivered is
abandoned); releasing too late violates \eqref{eq:c2} (the node keeps paying for dense data after
they have become undeliverable, and discharges fatally). The whole difficulty is that the two
state types observe $\trel$ very differently.

\paragraph{Record sub-problem: $\trel$ is locally certain.}
Because $d(s)$ uses only the public cadence, the node evaluates $\trel(s)=d(s)$ with certainty and
no downlink. Constraint~\eqref{eq:c1} is the exact test $d(s)\le t$, and \textsc{release} frees
both the backup send and the access batch. The optimal local rule is ordinary per-record expiry;
\S\ref{sec:mech-record} shows a certificate-keyed variant is the same rule and that the only design
choice with measurable consequence is \emph{where the release is executed}, via constraint
\eqref{eq:c3} and the cross-segment acknowledgement coupling.

\paragraph{Configuration sub-problem: $\trel$ is not locally observable.}
During $\mathcal O$ the node cannot compute $\trel(q)$ from \eqref{eq:trelcfg}: mission revisions
that change which obligations $q$ serves travel on the failed backhaul, and return-slot geometry
sits at the gateway. Three local approximations are available, each flawed:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,nosep]
\item \emph{Clock guard}: revert at sunset. Correct when $\mathcal O$ aligns with dusk and dense
data are undeliverable by then; blind to a daytime downgrade and to overcast days with little
harvest (\S\ref{sec:eval-lease}).
\item \emph{Fixed config TTL}: revert $\Delta$ after install. A single $\Delta$ cannot track
\eqref{eq:trelcfg}: short $\Delta$ violates \eqref{eq:c1} when the dense window is long; long
$\Delta$ violates \eqref{eq:c2} under tight energy when the window is short (\S\ref{sec:eval-lease}).
\item \emph{Local energy guard}: revert on a SoC threshold computed at the node. Necessary and
deployed as a safety net, but reactive---it acts only once energy is already low and cannot tell
whether the data being sacrificed were still deliverable.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Delivery-bounded lease (our mechanism).} While connected, the gateway computes
\eqref{eq:trelcfg} for the profile it is about to install, using obligations it knows and the
primary/backup slot schedule, and attaches the resulting absolute bound $\tau_L$ to the configuration
command. The node stores $\tau_L$ at apply time and reverts locally at $t\geq\tau_L$ with no
downlink. The bound uses only information valid at install time and never forecasts a future
authorisation; if a later connected revision changes the profile, a fresh command carries a fresh
lease. This is the ordinary config-lease/TTL primitive with its bound re-derived from delivery
geometry, exactly as semantic HARQ reuses HARQ but changes its criterion~\cite{razavivideo}: it is
the minimal local object that makes \eqref{eq:c1}--\eqref{eq:c2} jointly satisfiable without
assuming a surviving control path. When even the lease cannot keep the node alive, the honest
outcome under \eqref{eq:c4} is to revert to the safe sparse default and mark the interval's
obligations unfulfillable---not to report them complete.

\paragraph{Why this is a communication problem, not an agent problem.}
Problem~1 is defined over network state, evidence locality, and return geometry; it has an optimal
oracle solution and ordinary rule-based solutions independent of any LLM. An agent is one possible
\emph{solver} of the same decision (and a convenient probe of where the interface misleads a
planner); nothing in the objective or constraints references a model. We therefore evaluate the
mechanisms deterministically and treat the LLM study as formative.

\section{Mechanisms}
\label{sec:mech}

\subsection{Record termination: deadline-aligned expiry}
\label{sec:mech-record}
At each upload opportunity the node deletes cached records with $d(s)\le t$ and sends survivors
oldest-first. We implement two formulations: \texttt{deadline-purge}, keyed to the obligation
cadence, and \texttt{generic-expiry}, an ordinary BPv7-style per-record lifetime assigned at
creation, $\text{expires}=t_s+((P-t_s\bmod P)+P)=d(s)$, referencing no obligation id, slot
geometry, or certificate. A gateway variant locally acknowledges records it will not send. EDF and
obligation-greedy queues only reorder and never release; AoI \texttt{latest-only} releases
indiscriminately (keeps only the newest). Dropping stale/expired traffic is well established in AoI
packet management and DTN bundle expiry~\cite{costa2014pm,kam2016deadline,dtnarch,rfc9171}; the
sensor-transport practice of suppressing traffic whose delay bound is exceeded is likewise
prior art~\cite{wada2009timeout}. We claim no new discard algorithm. The system-level content is
the end-to-end business-deadline basis (not packet age, hop TTL, or an estimated single-packet
delay), zero-downlink source placement, and---decisively---cross-segment consistency, because the
acknowledgement coupling means a gateway-only release does not free the source cache.

\subsection{Configuration termination: local guard and delivery-bounded lease}
\label{sec:mech-lease}
\textbf{Local clock/energy guard (mature).} On every tick the node, on its local clock, clamps
sampling/report to sparse at night (hod $<06{:}00$ or $\geq18{:}00$) and leaves daytime commands
untouched; an optional computed energy ledger (remaining dark hours $\times$ the dense-vs-sparse
extra draw versus the node's own SoC) additionally reverts when dense operation would not survive
the night. This is a standard time/energy local rule and models the gateway's ``pre-provisioned
autonomous rules''; it is off by default and bit-identical when off.
\textbf{Delivery-bounded lease (the residual mechanism).} The gateway computes $\tau_L$
(Eq.~\ref{eq:trelcfg}) while connected: for the current dense profile it takes the last obligation
the profile produces before the next known revision, sets that obligation's qualified-sample and
return-slot deadline, aligns to the latest feasible primary/backup slot, and attaches $\tau_L$ to
the install command. The node enforces revert at $\tau_L$ alongside the clock guard; the guard
remains the always-on safety net for conditions the install-time bound cannot see (e.g.\ an
unforeseen weather collapse). A pre-provisioned full schedule is the degenerate case in which
$\tau_L$ for every segment is known at $t{=}0$ and a local schedule executor suffices with zero
centre traffic.

\subsection{Partial-observability projector and statement discipline}
\label{sec:mech-projector}
At compile time the centre holds the obligation table, the \emph{current} (never forecast)
network-side outage state, and public parameters; at mission-table arrival the gateway holds only
what it has heard. A structural no-return certificate (\stime) is emitted from slot geometry alone
and is explicitly conditional on the primary being unavailable; geometry never infers an outage.
An arrival-time projector labels a missed obligation only when local evidence separates the stages
and otherwise returns unknown. An unknown-aware centre projector emits \emph{unreachable} solely
from an explicit network-side field (which the current harness does not yet provide, so it emits
\emph{unconfirmed}), and replaces any blanket night kill with the computed energy ledger so that
genuine energy-infeasibility is distinguished from conservative advice.

\section{Prototype and methodology}
\label{sec:proto}
All mechanisms run in one Python event-stepped simulator (60~s ticks) implementing LoRaWAN Class~A
downlink windows, versioned two-field configuration commands (sampling interval and reporting
period as independently delivered fields, with cross-generation mixed configs detected), the
hold-until-acknowledged edge cache, max-coverage backup packing, stochastic solar/cloud, absorbing
brownout, and the non-prescient piecewise mission-view gate. Disabling the backup reproduces the
v1.1 simulator bit-for-bit (five anchored invariants); the local guard and lease are default-off.
\textbf{Two controlled episodes} are used. \emph{Episode~I} (the v1.1 manifest point): upgrade to
yellow at $t{=}6$~h, primary outage 4--20~h, the mission table reaching the gateway at 20~h; it
drives the frontier, attribution, record-expiry, placement, and live-agent results. \emph{Episode~II}
(a deterministic configuration-termination scenario, identical parameters except schedule,
outage phasing, and swept harvest): to place a daytime downgrade \emph{inside} an outage it uses
yellow at $t{=}2$~h with a conferred downgrade at $t{=}6$~h and outage 4--20~h (phase~A), or yellow
at $t{=}1$~h, downgrade at $t{=}8$~h, outage 6--22~h (phase~B); $t{=}12$~h is sunset. Episode~II is
an explicitly constructed controlled comparison for Problem~1's configuration sub-problem, not a
new field claim. We report node deaths (deterministic/highly robust) as totals over seeds 0--2 and
treat sub-point mean-service differences at $n{=}3$ as not significant; seeds 6--9 are a reused
held-out confirmation set, not an untouched test set. Every figure is regenerated by a registered
repository script and the suite reports 18/18 self-checks.

\section{Evaluation}

\subsection{Where the fixed-resource loss actually is}
\label{sec:eval-frontier}
Over Episode~I's 7560 routine obligations the strong maxcov/FIFO baseline delivers 3025 (0.400).
Symmetric per-obligation counterfactuals widen one physical resource at a time (never the policy)

establish that the large throughput gains are physical, which is precisely why we then target
\emph{state termination} rather than a smarter scheduler.

\paragraph{Full-horizon, time-aware failure attribution.}
Charging each of the 4535 failures to its first failing segment with ``heard'' required

\subsection{Record expiry: standard primitive, decisive placement}
\label{sec:eval-record}
During the Episode~I outage 75 backup packets carry 750 records; under FIFO 219 records arrive
centrally already expired versus 261 on-time (45.6\%; 1270 of 2782 payload bytes). Gateway-only
suppression removes those sends but withholds the acknowledgement, and on-time delivery falls
202$\to$194: cost is moved to the access segment. Source-local deadline expiry reverses the sign,
raising outage on-time delivery 202$\to$434 and the deadline-valid fraction 0.54$\to$1.00. Across
ten seeds (Table~\ref{tab:purgeexpiry}), expiry raises mean service 0.373$\to$0.415---a paired
$+4.21$ points (95\% CI $+3.64$ to $+4.79$, positive on all ten)---and total outage on-time
delivery 1691$\to$4265 (2.52$\times$), expired backup records 2538$\to$1, and deaths 12$\to$0; it
beats AoI latest-only by $+5.73$ points (CI $+5.06$ to $+6.39$), because latest-only buys outage
freshness by abandoning post-recovery coverage. Crucially, \texttt{deadline-purge} and
\texttt{generic-expiry} are bit-identical on every seed (service, outage counts, deaths, and the
delivered set): the record mechanism \emph{is} standard per-record expiration, and its measured
value is the business-deadline basis plus source/gateway cross-segment consistency, not a new
discard algorithm.

\subsection{Configuration leases: deaths under overcast skies, false release under short TTL}
\label{sec:eval-lease}
We now hold record expiry fixed (generic-expiry + maxcov) and vary only the configuration
termination in Episode~II. The node profile trace confirms the clock guard holds dense until
sunset ($t{=}12$~h): 12 samples/h in $[2,12)$~h and 6/h thereafter.

\paragraph{The delivery bound and a no-waste point.}
In phase~A the last yellow obligation is released just before the noon downgrade; its deadline,
aligned to a 1200~s backup slot, gives $\tau_L{=}6.33$~h. Sweeping the local revert time
(Table~\ref{tab:lease}), reverting at $\tau_L{=}6$~h removes 468 of 978 (48\%) post-deadline daytime
dense samples with \emph{zero} loss of yellow delivery (356/672) and no deaths; reverting as early
as 5~h falsely releases 9 yellow obligations, marking the no-false-release boundary. At nominal
harvest ($\eta{=}0.03$) service, deaths (0), and final SoC are unchanged: in this regime the lease
buys resource/airtime efficiency, not service.

\paragraph{Tight energy turns the lag into service loss.}
Sweeping harvest (3 seeds): for $\eta=0.03$, $0.02$, and $0.015$, every bound gives 0 deaths and service
$\approx0.597$. At the overcast $\eta{=}0.012$ the clock guard's sunset lag kills 20 nodes
(service 0.5961, final SoC 0.066) while the lease at 6--8~h removes all 20 deaths (0.5970, SoC
0.122, near-doubled); reverting as late as 10~h also avoids deaths but at a smaller margin (SoC
0.117). At $\eta{=}0.01$ even sparse operation cannot be powered ($\approx$40 deaths under every
bound); the lease modestly raises pre-death service (0.530$\to$0.577) but cannot save nodes---the
correct output is an unfulfillable declaration, not a false completion.

\paragraph{No fixed TTL is safe across phases.}
Phase~B needs the bound near 8.3~h (the long dense window), where a 4~h TTL relaxes at 5~h and
falsely releases 183 yellow obligations (service $-1.1$ points) and a 6~h TTL still loses 26;
phase~A needs it near 6.3~h, where an 8~h TTL only just survives at the tightest energy (SoC 0.117).
The same pattern holds at nominal harvest (the 4~h TTL still loses 183 obligations). The delivery
bound attains each phase's optimum automatically because it is computed from obligations and slots
rather than guessed as a duration. This is the mechanism's measured increment over a plain
config TTL: phase/rate-adaptive termination under constraints \eqref{eq:c1}--\eqref{eq:c2}, with a
bound that is auditable (``stop because the data cease to be deliverable here'') rather than
empirical. We report it as a point-specific, deterministic result over the tested phases; a wider
sweep of backup rates and node counts is future work.

\subsection{What the field can attribute when the table finally arrives}

yellow obligations are past deadline. Offline time-aware attribution labels all 2136 correctly

holds by $t_a$ labels 1138/2136 (53.3\%)---all geometric \stime\ (588), all in-time-not-returned

\subsection{Formative real-agent study}
\label{sec:eval-agent}
We ran deepseek-flash live over the Episode~I outage: three seeds each of an unaided agent (A0),
the agent with an ordinary pending-command checklist (A0s), and a certificate agent (A1), plus two
healthy-link controls, with a real model call at each 30-minute decision (99 per trace, 1089
decisions total); all arms share harness, legal observation, tools, history, and action space.
This study motivates and stress-tests the interface, and is not used to establish the deterministic
mechanisms.

\paragraph{Service and survival (noisy at $n{=}3$).} A1 attains mean service 0.358 (level with the
0.360 deterministic safe floor) with 4 deaths; A0 0.296 with 35 deaths and A0s 0.222 with 39; the
healthy-link controls give A0 0.275/13 deaths and A1 0.511/0. Per-seed service overlaps, so we draw
no service significance. The live agent loop was not wired to the local guard, so the four A1
seed-1 deaths are not cleared by the deterministic RQ of \S\ref{sec:eval-place}.

\paragraph{Accurate call accounting.} The 1089 decisions are not 1089 successful calls: 52 ended
in a parse-failure hold from \texttt{max\_tokens} cutting the JSON mid-string, and 412 records
carry a first-attempt error even when the retry succeeded (a logging artefact). Per-arm
parse-failure rates are A0 7.1\% (28/396), A0s 6.4\% (19/297), A1 1.3\% (5/396), so arm
differences mix output-format reliability with any semantic effect.

\paragraph{The interface fault, symmetrically scored.} The observation's \texttt{link} field
carries only LoRa access receipts (\texttt{n\_heard}) and no backhaul state. In the forced outage
window, A0/A0s assert ``link up / commands can apply'' on 36/32 decisions (A1 on 1 after
token-boundary de-noising of an ``uplink-only'' substring)---deterministically over-optimistic,
since the primary is down throughout; these accompany the ineffective dense orders and deaths. In
the opposite direction, the v4 certificate asserted ``control plane not delivering'' whenever
fewer than all nodes were heard, firing at five healthy-link times when the primary was in fact
usable, twice after 13/14 nodes had already confirmed dense. The earlier ``false-report'' tally was
invalid on three counts: it only ran on decisions that ordered dense (vacuous for the mostly-hold
A1), never ran on the healthy group (initialisation zeros), and counted keywords the certificate
prompt itself taught (such terms appear in 317 A1 decisions and zero A0/A0s decisions). Offline
replay of an unknown-aware v5 projector on the identical snapshots issues zero unreachable
assertions without a network-side field (all five healthy false alarms become unconfirmed) and
splits 528 blanket night kills into 189 genuinely energy-infeasible and 339 advisory (every
near-dawn 04:30--06:00 case is advisory). The honest finding is that a missing backhaul-state
signal causes bidirectional errors; isolating any independent increment of the projector requires
a matched-capability arm that receives the same constants and heuristics without the projector,
end-to-end v5 runs with an explicit backhaul-state field, and cross-model replication, all left as
future work.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\textbf{LLMs in emergency and network control.} TopoLLM applies adaptive tool learning to
post-disaster topology planning~\cite{topollm}; later systems let LLMs schedule UAV paths and
bandwidth, shape RL rewards, or orchestrate recovery tools, including intent objects, budget/time
constraints, candidate-plan checks, local repair, and documented network-management
APIs~\cite{icgrestore,ossgpt}. We therefore claim no novelty for ``natural language to
constraints'' or tool orchestration. These decide network resources or compose recovery tools
under an assumed reachable control path; they do not terminate state that must keep acting after
that path dies, and do not maintain an unknown-aware three-class state over a long routine
monitoring workflow. Agentic Wi-Fi likewise uses an LLM as a solver for a separately defined
coordination protocol; we follow that ordering---problem first, agent optional.

\textbf{Scheduling, freshness, energy.} The closest state-model precedent is an LLM in-context
data-collection scheduler with bounded per-sensor queues, exogenous arrivals, finite service
opportunities, and feedback~\cite{icldc}; AoI and energy-harvesting control, including
instantaneous-battery threshold policies and partially observed harvesting, address resource
pieces~\cite{bacinoglu1905,zakeri2311,costa2014pm}; overbooking and contact-aware forwarding for
space links~\cite{ccsdssabr} and network-calculus bounds~\cite{leboudec01} are complementary
deterministic characterisations. Joint sampling/transmission/energy optimisation is established
and we do not claim it; our distinction is persistent \emph{remote} configuration with Class~A
apply latency, a separated access vs.\ primary/backup return path, and termination under a control
outage. Consistent network update research has long studied per-update validity, dependencies,
and safe transitions~\cite{sdnconsistency}; we rely on that lesson (versioning and dependencies
are not new) and add the disconnected, energy-constrained edge case in which an installed update's
\emph{own data} become undeliverable before a counter-update can arrive.

\textbf{Packet discard, DTN expiry, contacts.} AoI management pre-empts or drops old
updates~\cite{kam2016deadline,krishnan2019multihop}; deadline-aware buffer discard has been
coupled with adaptive ARQ for video~\cite{razavivideo}; DTN bundle protocols attach creation-time
lifetimes and use TTL/encounter drop~\cite{dtnarch,iranmaneshdtnqueue,rfc9171}, and custody
transfer~\cite{rfc5050} moves retention responsibility as a forward hand-off (since migrated out
of the BPv7 core). Rural LoRa--LEO systems schedule around estimated satellite contacts and
exploit spatial gateway diversity~\cite{sateriot}. Our record disposition \emph{is} BPv7-style
expiry and we prove the equivalence; our configuration lease is a TTL/lease primitive with a
delivery-derived bound. The claimed deltas are narrow and explicit: an end-to-end business-deadline
basis with cross-segment source/gateway consistency (expired backhaul suppression alone
back-pressures access), and a configuration bound computed from the last deliverable slot so that
termination is safe across outage phases without a live control path.

\section{Discussion, limitations, and threats to validity}
\label{sec:disc}
\textbf{Scope.} The throughput negative result and the resource walls are scoped to the policy
families and one operating point; they are not an optimum, and a better scheduler could exist. The
lease result is deterministic over two constructed phases and five harvest levels; it establishes
the mechanism's existence and the failure of fixed clock/TTL bounds across those phases, not a
universal parameter choice. Wider sweeps of backup rate/payload, node count, capacity, cloud
model, restart thresholds, and non-absorbing brownout are future work and could move the energy
wall (not the slot/capacity walls). The lease bound uses information valid at install time; an
authorisation changed entirely during a long outage is physically unreachable, and the correct
behaviour is safe-default revert plus an unfulfillable declaration, which we report rather than
hide.
\textbf{Model simplifications.} Central acknowledgement is synchronous and airtime-free, equally
favouring every queue rule; gateway acknowledgements would consume Class~A windows in deployment.
During Episode~I the node tests deadlines against the public pre-change cadence, with post-upgrade
deadlines used only in offline accounting. Single fixed-period missions make obligation-keyed and
deadline-keyed record release coincide; multi-obligation or overlapping mission versions require
release keyed to a record's latest still-valid deadline. Episode~II is a controlled scenario for
the configuration sub-problem, not an independent field measurement.
\textbf{Agent evidence.} The live study is single-model, three seeds per arm, temperature-0, with
large per-run variance and a confounded expert packet; it demonstrates the interface fault and the
projector's offline effect, not an independent agent increment. The hardened harness (per-request
accounting, symmetric scoring, unknown-aware projector and energy ledger, default-off local guard
and lease) is in the repository so matched-capability, end-to-end v5, and cross-model studies can
run without re-litigating instrumentation; those are explicitly not claimed here.
\textbf{Provenance.} One standards channel was obtained from a document-sharing host (top-grade
document, lower-grade copy chain) because the official host was unreachable by TLS; clause numbers
were cross-checked from two independent copies, and unverified international LoRa-plus-satellite
and vendor lines are not used as fact. We did not execute cited systems' implementations and claim
no replication of them.

\section{Conclusion}
After a backhaul failure, the communication state installed beforehand keeps executing, and its
termination cannot wait for the control path that failed. We formulated this as one local
termination problem over two kinds of persistent installed state. Records carry a locally certain
release time---the business deadline---and standard per-record expiry, placed consistently at the
source, doubles outage on-time delivery while gateway-only suppression merely moves cost to the
access segment. Configurations carry a release time the node cannot observe during an outage; a
fixed clock guard or fixed TTL either kills nodes under overcast skies or falsely releases
deliverable obligations across phases, whereas a delivery-bounded lease---an ordinary lease whose
bound is the last deliverable slot, installed while connected and enforced locally---removes those
deaths without false release. Throughout, mechanisms act only on evidence the acting entity
lawfully holds, say unknown when they cannot tell, and declare work unfulfillable rather than
complete. The network problem and its deterministic solutions stand on their own; an LLM is one
candidate controller, and our live study shows most clearly the interface fault---access receipts
mistaken for backhaul state---that the evidence discipline is designed to prevent.
