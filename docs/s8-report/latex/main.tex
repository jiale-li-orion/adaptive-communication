\begin{abstract}
We study whether an agent runtime placed \emph{above} the communication entities of a
pre-disaster landslide monitoring deployment can keep a monitoring mission executing when
field nodes, the LoRaWAN access, and the cellular/short-message backhaul dynamically appear,
degrade, disconnect, and recover. Under a source-grounded mountain deployment (battery+solar
Class~A nodes, a gateway with local autonomy, a 16~h cellular outage, and a sparse BeiDou
short-message backup), we first establish a \emph{symmetric feasibility frontier}: with
strong reactive baselines (energy-aware sampling, obligation-driven max-coverage backup
packing, piecewise mission views, FIFO repair), per-obligation counterfactual relaxations and
an omniscient offline packing show that a controller---including a joint optimizer or an
LLM---cannot raise on-time delivery by re-optimising collection, scheduling, or packing under the
standard edge discipline, without changing resources or the granted authority (the one
fixed-resource gain is an orthogonal execution-layer change to record disposition, shown later);
the residual failures decompose cleanly into four chain segments. We then show that the
genuine, achievable value is \emph{feasibility truth}, not throughput. At mission-compile
time, the geometry of the backup schedule alone lets us certify, with 100\% recall and zero
false positives a median of 7.2~h ahead, the obligations whose delivery window contains no
return path; when the mission table finally traverses the outage, the gateway attributes the
correct failure segment for 100\% of already-missed obligations from its local log. We further
find that 45.6\% of short-message records delivered during the outage arrive already expired.
Critically, feasibility truth also has an \emph{execution-layer payoff}. Under the standard link
semantics in which a record is cleared from a node's FIFO repair cache only once the centre
acknowledges it, naively withholding expired backup records is not free: the unacknowledged dead
records keep occupying each limited access batch and push fresh samples' reception past the
outage---a cross-layer transfer of cost from the backhaul to the access segment. A
\emph{deadline-aware selective purge}, the execution-layer counterpart of the certificate, stops
holding and retransmitting only records whose obligation deadline has passed while retaining
every still-rescuable record; it is implementable at the node with zero downlink signalling. At
the main point it raises outage on-time delivery from 202 to 434 and full-horizon service from
0.400 to 0.432 under unchanged resources, drives expired sends to zero (send-promise precision
$0.54\!\to\!1.00$), and on three held-out seeds raises service by 3.7--5.6 points and outage
delivery 2.2--3.4$\times$. It dominates the AoI latest-only queue, which matches outage freshness
only by discarding still-useful records (service 0.363); EDF and obligation-greedy queues that
reorder but never release gain nothing.
In a within-subject study with a real LLM controller, the unaided agent mistakes LoRa access
for backhaul connectivity, storms ineffective commands, and silently reports the elevated
mission as in effect; given only the certificate, the same agent stops, locates the blocking
segment, holds the last lawful configuration, and initiates a standards-authorised
re-compilation. The contribution is an execution-grounded obligation model, segment-labelled
feasibility certificates, an execution-layer selective purge that turns a certified loss into
both metered-cost savings and higher on-time delivery without adding resources, and lawful
re-compilation that align what the system promises with what it can actually deliver.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{Where the agent sits.}
Prior emergency-communication research combines and recovers \emph{given} network roles and
infrastructure: it places UAVs, allocates bandwidth, re-plans topology, or sequences
infrastructure recovery tools. We instead place an agent runtime \emph{above} these entities
and study its ability to sustain task execution as the entities themselves change state. The
setting is pre-disaster monitoring in a mountainous geohazard area, under tight energy and
intermittent access/backhaul. The reference deployment keeps field nodes, a gateway with a
cache and pre-provisioned autonomous rules, and intermittent backhaul; LoRaWAN Class~A is the
studied access. Relay and backup backhaul enter only where hardware and standards support
them.

\paragraph{What we expected, and what we found.}
An early hypothesis was that an agent that jointly configures collection and backup sending
would deliver more than fixed rules. A sequence of increasingly strong, symmetric tests
refuted the \emph{throughput} version of that hypothesis, and---importantly---refuted it
without rigging the baseline. Under fixed resources, strong reactive policies already sit on
the feasible frontier; the failures that remain are physical walls (no return slot, backup
capacity, night-time energy), and omniscient offline packing cannot route around them. This bound
concerns collection, scheduling, and packing decisions under the standard hold-until-acknowledged
edge discipline; we later find one fixed-resource gain orthogonal to it, an execution-layer change
to how already-dead records are disposed (\S\ref{sec:purge}).

Closing the throughput question did not close the research problem. Instrumenting a real LLM
controller exposed a different, sharper failure: the runtime is \emph{blind to where the
mission breaks}. During a 16~h backhaul outage it read ``nodes are heard over LoRa'' as
``backhaul is up'', kept emitting the elevated 300~s configuration (606 commands refused, 14
confirmed), oscillated between configurations every 30~minutes, and reported the elevated
mission as running while the field still executed the previous, lower alert level. The same
aggregate telemetry (age-of-information, state-of-charge) cannot tell the controller whether
data were never sampled, sit at a node, sit at the gateway, or lack a return opportunity, and
cannot distinguish a command queued, in transit, or applied.

\paragraph{The reframed problem.}
The controller should compile a changing, authorised monitoring requirement into an
\emph{executable plan} over sampling, access, energy, and return opportunities, and---crucially---when a part of the mission cannot be honoured, it must say \emph{which obligation,
in which time interval, at which chain segment, is infeasible, and what lawful change would
move it}, rather than silently under-delivering or killing nodes to try.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Obligation-chain model and symmetric feasibility frontier (\S\ref{sec:frontier}).}
A per-obligation chain (qualified sample $\to$ gateway $\to$ return before deadline $\to$
central evidence) and a methodology of symmetric per-obligation counterfactual relaxations.
Strong baselines attain the frontier; under fixed resources no controller (rule, joint MPC,
or omniscient packing) has a realisable on-time delivery gain. Failures decompose into four
segments with zero unexplained remainder.
\item \textbf{Execution-grounded feasibility certificates (\S\ref{sec:cert}).}
Compile-time structural certificates (no return slot; mission/command unreachable) that are
100\% sensitive with zero false positives and a 7.2~h median lead, plus arrival-time segment
attribution at 100\% once the delayed mission table reaches the field. We delimit exactly
which claims are deterministic and which require online state.
\item \textbf{Lawful re-compilation and real cost (\S\ref{sec:cert},\S\ref{sec:eval}).}
Re-compilation confined to standards-granted authority (remote frequency adjustment;
conference upgrade/downgrade), with measurable cost accounting: 45.6\% of outage short-message
records arrive expired (a record-level waste upper bound), and certificate-aware holding avoids
futile night-time dense sampling that permanently kills nodes.
\item \textbf{Execution-layer certificate: deadline-aware selective purge (\S\ref{sec:purge},\S\ref{sec:eval}).}
The same feasibility judgement, applied at the edge, releases from the FIFO repair cache only
records proven past their deadline and retains every rescuable one. Under fixed resources it more
than doubles outage on-time delivery (202$\to$434) and raises full-horizon service 3.2 points at
the main point (3.7--5.6 on held-out seeds), with zero expired sends and send-promise precision
$0.54\!\to\!1.00$. It is location-independent (a zero-signalling node queue slightly beats a
gateway version), dominates AoI latest-only, and is not reproduced by EDF or obligation-greedy
queues.
\item \textbf{Real-agent within-subject evidence (\S\ref{sec:agent}.)}
With identical legal information, adding only the certificate converts a real LLM
controller's segment misdiagnosis, command storm, and silent mis-reporting into
segment-correct, lawful, honest behaviour across three decision points.
\end{enumerate}
We do \emph{not} claim an LLM out-optimises a solver with the full task model; the deterministic
projector, packer, and energy ledger are in-layer tools available to rules and agents alike.

\section{Background, scenario, and standards grounding}
\label{sec:bg}

\paragraph{Deployment.}
Fourteen sensor nodes in two groups monitor displacement (and rainfall) on a slope. Nodes
sample and upload to a local gateway over LoRaWAN Class~A; the gateway forwards to a central
server over cellular backhaul, with a BeiDou short-message service as a sparse backup. Nodes
are battery plus small solar. The mission is \emph{pre-disaster} and routine, with authorised
warning-level changes; the agent never judges landslide risk.

\paragraph{Standards-grounded authority.}
We use two Chinese geological-hazard monitoring industry standards, DZ/T~0460--2023 and
DZ/T~0450--2023, retrieved and clause-audited in the repository~\cite{dzt0460,dzt0450}. Relevant clauses:
\S5.3.3 permits \emph{remote dynamic adjustment of sampling and upload frequency by warning
level} (no numeric levels are given, so the periods we use are declared research choices);
\S8.4.1 defines four levels (red/orange/yellow/blue) and \S8.4.2 permits upgrade, downgrade,
or termination after a conference; \S5.3.7 calls for dual-mode communication under weak
coverage in high-risk extreme weather (a conditional requirement, appearing once); \S5.3.6
gives endurance targets. The BeiDou open-service specification (BDS-OS-PS-3.0)~\cite{bdsps3} reports
$\geq$95\% success, average latency under 2~s, an average service frequency of one message per
30~s, and a single-message ceiling of 14{,}000 bits, while noting that realised frequency and
maximum length are constrained by registration parameters---i.e.\ the rate is a
\emph{provisioned} quantity. These clauses bound what the agent may lawfully do: it may
re-compile frequency within granted levels and request a conference change, but it may not
silently drop an obligation or declare risk itself.

\paragraph{Why the numbers are what they are.}
The short-message service is metered and sparse; the studied operating point uses one
78-byte packet every 1200~s (20~B header, 58~B payload; a displacement sample is 6~B, so
roughly 9--10 samples fit per packet). The elevated (yellow) task uses a 300~s sampling period
with half-open windows and a delivery deadline two periods after window open, i.e.\ a
$\approx$600~s delivery window---\emph{shorter} than the 1200~s backup slot spacing. This
geometric mismatch is load-bearing for our structural certificate.

\section{System model and the obligation chain}
\label{sec:model}

Time is slotted at 60~s. A \textbf{monitoring obligation} $o$ is a tuple
$(n,m,[l_o,h_o),d_o)$ over node $n$, measurand $m$, sampling window $[l_o,h_o)$ and absolute
delivery deadline $d_o$. A routine obligation at period $P$ has $h_o-l_o=P$ and
$d_o=h_o+P$ (one period of grace). We track a sample through a fixed chain:
\[
\underbrace{\text{qualified sample taken}}_{\text{node}}
\;\to\;
\underbrace{\text{heard at gateway}}_{\text{LoRa access}}
\;\to\;
\underbrace{\text{returned before }d_o}_{\text{cellular / short-message}}
\;\to\;
\underbrace{\text{central evidence}}_{\text{mission fulfilled}} .
\]
An obligation is \emph{fulfilled} only when a sample matching $(n,m)$ is taken in-window and
received centrally by $d_o$; matching uses half-open windows so a boundary sample cannot
double-cover adjacent windows.

\paragraph{Energy and devices.}
A sample costs $4.7{\times}10^{-4}$~Wh; an uplink $2.33{\times}10^{-5}$~Wh. Battery capacity
is 0.05~Wh. Solar input follows a 12~h half-sine with peak 0.03~Wh/h, with per-node/hour
stochastic cloud occlusion (35\% chance of attenuation to 0.25; expected coefficient
$k_c\!\approx\!0.7375$). A node that browns out \emph{permanently} dies in the main
configuration (an absorbing state; a restart sensitivity is left open). A cache of 240 slots
with FIFO replacement sits at the node. A record is removed from this cache only once it is
acknowledged at the centre (the acknowledgement is modelled synchronously and without airtime,
a pre-existing simplification that favours every queue discipline equally); until then it is
retransmitted oldest-first, with at most 32 records per upload opportunity. Access succeeds
with probability 0.74; primary backhaul is good with probability 0.62.

\paragraph{Class~A execution reality.}
A configuration command is not immediate: it is queued, transits the backhaul to the gateway,
and reaches a node only in its next Class~A receive window; it counts as \textsc{applied}
only when the node later reports the new config. During a backhaul outage, commands are
refused and fresh telemetry stops. A command issued now, applied later, and still consuming
energy in between is a first-class effect; dense sampling across a 12~h night needs
$\approx$0.071~Wh, exceeding the 0.05~Wh battery.

\paragraph{Mission reachability.}
The authorised schedule is piecewise (blue 600~s, then yellow 300~s from $t{=}6$~h). Crucially,
the \emph{mission table itself} must traverse the backhaul to the gateway; in the main
scenario the primary is down from $t{=}4$~h to $t{=}20$~h, and the table (which does not use
the short-message path) reaches the gateway only at $t{=}20$~h. Before that instant the field
lawfully continues the last \textsc{applied} blue configuration under local autonomy. This is
not a modelling convenience: it is the phenomenon we study.

\paragraph{Strong baselines (the bar to beat).}
We assemble deliberately strong ordinary mechanisms, each given the same legal information:
\textbf{dayfeed}, an energy-aware sampling policy that densifies by day and relaxes through
the night (the implicit worst-case-robust reaction); \textbf{maxcov}, a textbook
max-coverage/recency greedy that packs each backup packet by marginal new obligation coverage
per byte; \textbf{piecewise mission views} with a gateway gate that withholds future
requirements until they actually arrive, while preserving already-open windows across a
level change; and \textbf{FIFO} access repair. A scripted dayfeed decider driven through the
full agent harness reproduces the reference service level bit-for-bit, demonstrating no
hidden privilege or penalty for the agent path.

\section{The symmetric feasibility frontier}
\label{sec:frontier}

Before proposing any mechanism we ask whether controllable, changeable loss exists. We do so
with \emph{symmetric} per-obligation counterfactuals: each relaxation widens one physical
resource (never the policy), and we report set differences over the same obligation ids, so a
single strategy's good luck cannot masquerade as an upper bound.

\subsection{Failure-segment attribution}
For every unfulfilled obligation we attribute the first blocking segment using an
\emph{optimistic} model that grants every assumption favourable to feasibility:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,nosep]
\item \stime: even optimistically, no return opportunity (primary slot or backup slot) exists
between the qualified sample and $d_o$;
\item \senergy: a return opportunity exists but no qualified sample is collected;
\item \saccess: collected but never heard at the gateway;
\item \scap: collected and heard, but backup capacity / recovery congestion cannot return it
by $d_o$.
\end{itemize}
Only the first failing segment is charged. The optimistic model for return opportunities
counts deterministic backup slots and an open primary; an obligation is labelled \stime only
when even this favourable model finds no slot. Over the full 48~h, 7560 routine obligations,
the strong baseline delivers 3025 (0.400) with zero node deaths; the 4535 failures attribute as
\senergy\ 2002 (26.5\%), \scap\ 1945 (25.7\%), \stime\ 588 (7.8\%), \saccess\ 0.

\subsection{The sampling/energy subspace is already closed}
A feed-forward night-energy policy that ignores two costs kills 13 nodes (service 0.213): it
omits the Class~A command latency $L$ (the node keeps sampling densely while the relax command
propagates) and the dawn charging ramp $R$ (solar input is near zero at sunrise). Counting
both ($L{=}3600$, $R{=}7200$~s) collapses the policy \emph{bit-for-bit} to dayfeed. A
rolling-horizon MPC that integrates the true half-sine harvest gains 1.47 points on seed~0 but
averages $-1.3$ points over six seeds and introduces 21 new permanent deaths on seeds dayfeed
survives; a robust sweep over the cloud coefficient has no operating point that adds no deaths
and is not worse on average. The reason is structural: legal information does not reveal
tomorrow's clouds, while dense sampling under random cloud plus an absorbing brownout state
has asymmetric cost. Dayfeed's ``relax every night'' is an implicit minimax-robust response.

The first failure of an elevated task during an outage is therefore \textbf{mission/command
unreachability}, not energy and not packet selection. The field cannot execute a requirement
it never receives. An omniscient compile-time EDF packing that assumes every obligation's
sample is present at window start claims 420 obligations fit the backup slots, yet only 9 are
realised---the offline packing was optimistic about \emph{supply}, which the unreachable task
table prevents. Feasibility projection must take the field's legally knowable supply rhythm as
input, not the nominal mission table.

\paragraph{Frontier conclusion.} Across the full horizon and within the outage, the two
subspaces' omniscient upper bounds collapse onto the reactive baseline, and the only large
gains require adding backhaul capacity or energy. Under fixed resources and authority there is
no realisable throughput headroom for a controller, a joint MPC, or an LLM within collection,
scheduling, and packing under the fixed edge discipline. (The execution-layer record-disposition
action of \S\ref{sec:purge} was held constant in these relaxations and is examined separately.)
This is a strong,
symmetrically backed \emph{negative result} for the control question---and it frees the
contribution to live where value genuinely remains.

\section{Feasibility certificates and lawful re-compilation}
\label{sec:cert}

We give the runtime an obligation-level feasibility projector that emits three kinds of
statement, kept rigorously distinct: \textbf{fulfilled} (verified by an independent scorer),
\textbf{not currently guaranteed} (a knowledge boundary), and
\textbf{infeasible-under-stated-conditions} (which requires an optimistic model to also fail;
a heuristic that merely did not finish may never claim impossibility).

\subsection{Two placement levels}
\textbf{Compile-time (central),} when an authorised change is authored, the centre holds the
full obligation table, network-side outage status, and public device/link parameters. It runs
an optimistic allocation and emits only \emph{zero-false-positive structural} certificates.
\textbf{Arrival-time (gateway),} when a (possibly delayed) mission table arrives, the gateway
has the richest local state---heard samples, its own forwarding log, backup slots used---and
performs precise per-obligation attribution and near-horizon packing, while remaining
autonomous during outages. Capacity-contention outcomes that depend on online randomness are
\emph{not} asserted per-obligation at compile time.

\subsection{Structural no-return certificate ($S_{\mathrm{time}}$)}
Backup slots occur at $t\bmod 1200{=}0$; an upgraded obligation's delivery window is 600~s.
An obligation whose window contains no backup slot (and whose primary is in outage) cannot be
returned even under optimistic supply and unlimited node energy. This test uses only public
geometry and contains no random quantity, so it is seed-invariant.

\subsection{Mission-unreachable certificate}
If the mission table (and hence Class~A configuration commands) cannot reach the field before
an obligation's deadline, the field will execute the last applied configuration under local
autonomy; the elevated obligation is unreachable as a command, independent of node energy. The
centre may derive this from its own command ledger (queued/refused, no \textsc{applied}
confirmation) and network-side outage; no future truth is used.

\subsection{Arrival-time segment attribution}
When the delayed table arrives at $t{=}20$~h, the gateway replays each already-dead obligation
against its local heard/forwarding log and the public slot geometry, assigning
\stime/\scap/\senergy/\saccess.

\subsection{Lawful re-compilation}
For certified obligations the runtime acts only within standards authority: hold the last
lawful configuration and local autonomy; suppress futile commands and expired backup sends (the latter only paired with the selective
purge of \S\ref{sec:purge}, never by withholding sends alone);
log an obligation-level infeasibility certificate with the segment and the resource change
that would move it; after recovery, compile only the guaranteeable subset; and where the
mission truly requires more, request added backup quota or a conference downgrade/upgrade under
\S5.3.3/\S8.4.2. The agent never silently marks an unfulfilled obligation complete, never
deletes it, and never judges landslide risk.

\subsection{Execution-layer certificate: deadline-aware selective purge}
\label{sec:purge}
The compile-time certificate says an obligation cannot be returned; the execution-layer question
is what the edge should \emph{do} with records already caught in its caches. Two facts interact.
A record leaves a node's repair cache only on central acknowledgement; otherwise FIFO repair
keeps re-sending the oldest unacknowledged records, and during an outage the metered backup path
is the only route to such an acknowledgement.

\paragraph{Why naively suppressing expired sends is not free.}
Withholding a backup record that is already past its deadline (the obvious reading of the 45.6\%
waste) withholds its acknowledgement as well. The record then stays in the node cache and, as an
oldest unacknowledged entry, fills the limited access batches, so genuinely fresh samples are not
heard until the primary recovers. At the main point this naive suppression saves 29\% of backup
bytes but costs 8 on-time obligations and 0.25 points of service: cost is moved from the backhaul
segment to the access segment, not removed.

\paragraph{Selective purge.}
The execution-layer counterpart of the certificate releases an unacknowledged edge record
\emph{if and only if} its obligation deadline has already passed ($d\le t$ under the locally
known period)---a zero-false-positive test because the deadline is absolute---while retaining
every record with $d>t$. This both stops the expired backup send and frees the access batch. It
has two equivalent placements: a node-local queue discipline that needs no downlink at all
(\texttt{deadline-purge}), and a gateway action that locally acknowledges records it will not send
(\texttt{cert-purge}); both use only the public period $P$ legally available before the mission
table arrives. The two placements implement one deadline judgement and give the same result.

\paragraph{Why ordinary queues do not suffice.}
EDF and obligation-greedy disciplines reorder which records occupy a batch but never release
them, so the cache is not freed and we measure no gain. The AoI latest-only queue does release
records, but indiscriminately (it keeps only the newest), matching outage freshness while
permanently abandoning records still deliverable after recovery and lowering full-horizon
service. The certificate supplies the \emph{selectivity} that turns a blunt freshness heuristic
into a service-preserving action; this is an execution-layer mechanism a fixed rule realises, and
whose per-obligation judgements an agent must maintain when requirements change.

\paragraph{Relationship to the feasibility frontier.}
The negative result of \S\ref{sec:frontier} is scoped to collection, access scheduling, and
backhaul packing under the standard edge discipline that \emph{holds every unacknowledged record
until the centre confirms it}. Selective purge is an orthogonal execution-layer action: it changes
a record's \emph{lifetime disposition}, not resource use, and that disposition was held fixed in
every counterfactual relaxation. Its gain is therefore not throughput extracted from a physical
wall; it removes an internal leak in which records already proven unrecoverable keep consuming
access batches. The symmetric frontier bounds what better \emph{decisions} achieve given the
protocol's state handling; the execution-layer certificate corrects that state handling itself.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\textbf{Setup.} Seed~0 main point (with multi-seed checks where randomness enters): 48~h + 1~h
tail, 14 nodes, two groups, cap 0.05~Wh, solar peak 0.03~Wh/h, outage 4--20~h, backup
1200~s/78~B with maxcov, blue$\to$yellow (600$\to$300~s) at 6~h, mission table reaching the
gateway at 20~h. All numbers are produced by the repository scripts (r29--r36), each run
reproduces 18/18 repository checks and 5/5 joint invariants.

\subsection{RQ1: Do structural certificates provide early, precise warning?}
\textbf{Yes, and deterministically.} At compile time (6~h) the geometry test emits 602
no-slot certificates over the outage window; 588 obligations truly end as \stime (100\%
recall of the 588), with 14 boundary cases at the recovery edge attributed to \scap, and
\textbf{zero false positives} (no certified obligation is delivered). Lead time
$d_o-6\,$h ranges 0.2--14~h, median 7.2~h. By contrast, an optimistic-energy capacity
certificate is impure (552 correct \scap, 571 actually \senergy, 193 false positives), which
is precisely why we restrict compile-time claims to structural/unreachable segments and push
capacity attribution to the gateway.

\subsection{RQ2: Can the field attribute failures when the table finally arrives?}
\textbf{Yes, at 100\%.} 2136 yellow obligations are already past deadline when their table
arrives at 20~h. From its local complete log the gateway attributes the correct segment for
2136/2136 (true segments: \stime 588, \scap 977, \senergy 571). Deterministic \emph{advance}
certification before the deadline is essentially impossible at the field (4 marginal cases),
because the field physically does not know these obligations exist---the certificate value at
this level is immediate, segment-labelled accounting versus silent, un-explained loss.

\subsection{RQ3: Is real cost wasted on the infeasible, and can a certificate recover it?}
During the outage 75 backup packets carry 750 records (5848~B). Of 480 records actually
received centrally via backup during the outage, 261 are on-time and \textbf{219 (45.6\%) are
already expired} (1270 of 2782 payload bytes). The tempting response---simply stop sending expired records---is not free, for the
acknowledgement reason of \S\ref{sec:purge}. Table~\ref{tab:purge} holds the strong maxcov
backup chooser fixed and varies only the edge queue/action. Naive suppression removes the 219

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\textbf{LLMs in emergency / network control.} TopoLLM~\cite{topollm} uses an LLM for post-disaster rescue
topology and resource planning; subsequent lines let LLMs (i) directly schedule UAV
path/data collection and bandwidth, (ii) shape rewards for deep RL, or (iii) orchestrate
infrastructure-recovery tools (e.g.\ graph-guided candidate-plan repair for network
restoration and LLM-driven operation of real network-management
APIs)~\cite{icgrestore,ossgpt}. These decide \emph{network resources}
or compose recovery tools; they do not maintain a long-horizon routine monitoring workflow
across collection, configuration, return, and recovery under intermittent links and tight
energy, and they do not produce per-obligation feasibility certificates grounded in the
execution chain. Intent/graph-based restoration systems already include intent objects,
budget/time constraints, candidate-plan checks and local repair, and LLM systems already
orchestrate documented APIs~\cite{icgrestore,ossgpt}; we therefore do not claim ``natural
language to constraints'' as
novel. Our delta is the per-obligation chain projection, the segment-labelled infeasibility
certificate with zero-false-positive structural tests, and the compile-time/arrival-time
split under mission unreachability.

\textbf{Scheduling, freshness, energy.} Constrained-queue scheduling with exogenous arrivals
and finite service opportunities provides our formal anchors~\cite{icldc}; age-of-information
and energy-harvesting link optimisation---including instantaneous-battery threshold policies
and partially-observed energy-harvesting control~\cite{bacinoglu1905,zakeri2311}---and
overbooking/scheduled-aware forwarding for space links~\cite{ccsdssabr} address pieces of the
resource problem. We
use these as strong baselines and upper-bound tools, not as strawmen, and we do not claim
joint sampling/transmission/energy optimisation as novel. Our setting differs in remote
\emph{persistent} configuration with Class~A apply latency and in the separated access vs.\
primary/backup return process, and---decisively---in asking what the controller should
\emph{say and lawfully do} when the mission is structurally unfulfillable, rather than only
how to pack more bits. Consistent network update work has long studied
per-update validity, dependencies, and loop-freedom during transitions~\cite{sdnconsistency};
we rely on that lesson (versioning, dependencies, local repair are not new) and place
task-transition effects after it. Network calculus and delay/disruption-tolerant triage under
extreme contacts are complementary deterministic characterisations of the walls we
measure~\cite{leboudec01}.

\section{Discussion, limitations, and threats to validity}
\label{sec:disc}
\textbf{Scope of claims.} The throughput negative result is established at one main operating
point with symmetric relaxations and strong (including omniscient and MPC) baselines; the
structural \stime certificate is geometric and seed-invariant, but capacity and night-energy
\emph{forecasts} are deliberately not claimed as deterministic---they depend on online state
and unknown weather. The agent study is a three-point within-subject existence proof; an
effect-size claim requires 3--5 full trajectories and an A0-structured arm (plain engineering
checklists, pending-command lists, bookkeeping) to ensure the gain is not merely good
engineering hygiene. The brownout model is absorbing; a restart sensitivity could move the
energy wall but not the slot/capacity walls. The centre's knowledge of outage duration assumes
network-side operations visibility; we also give a conservative variant that assumes only the
current outage and counts deterministic backup slots. Standards text for one channel was
obtained from a document-sharing host (top-grade document, lower-grade copy chain) with the
official host unreachable by TLS from our environment; clause numbers were cross-checked from
two independent copies. We did not execute authors' reference implementations and therefore
do not claim replication of any cited system; one field study endpoint was rate-limited from
our network and is cited without its numbers.

\textbf{Selective-purge assumptions.} (i) The central acknowledgement is modelled synchronously
and without airtime, a pre-existing v1.1 simplification that favours every queue discipline
equally; the node-local \texttt{deadline-purge} needs no downlink at all and is our primary
mechanism, whereas the gateway \texttt{cert-purge}'s explicit acknowledgements would consume
Class~A receive windows in a real deployment. (ii) A purged record forgoes any retrospective value
after its deadline; where such value matters it can be retained at the gateway rather than sent.
(iii) During the outage the node and gateway test deadlines against the public pre-change period
$P{=}600$; the exact post-upgrade $P{=}300$ deadline enters only post-hoc accounting, never as an
online input. (iv) Edge-queue results use the development seed plus three held-out seeds; a full
seed 0--9 sweep with confidence intervals remains future work.

\textbf{Generality.} The obligation chain and the three-way statement discipline transfer to
any intermittent, energy-limited, authority-bounded monitoring workflow; the specific walls
are parameterised by the deployment and should be re-derived per deployment.

\section{Conclusion}
For pre-disaster geohazard monitoring under intermittent connectivity, strong ordinary
controllers already extract essentially all on-time delivery that fixed resources allow; the
remaining failures are physical and cleanly attributable. The defensible role for an agent
runtime is therefore not to out-pack the network but to maintain \emph{execution-grounded
honesty}: to compile authorised missions against the real collection/access/return/energy
chain, certify structural infeasibility hours ahead with no false alarms, attribute every
miss to its segment the moment evidence arrives, and---at the execution layer---release exactly
the records a certificate proves unrecoverable while keeping every rescuable one, so that honesty
about infeasibility also frees real on-time delivery rather than merely moving cost between the
access and backhaul segments; it then re-compiles or
re-negotiates only within the law---so that the system promises exactly what it can execute,
and says, precisely and early, why it cannot do the rest.
